# Reviewer C

## 1. Summary assessment

This manuscript proposes a selective, frame-level monitor that estimates an interaction preference value (IPV), abstains when the estimate or situation is unsupported, and compares accepted readings with a situation-conditioned human reference interval calibrated by split conformal prediction. It then relates assertive-side flags to subsequent interaction margins and counterpart responses in a matched-scenario vehicle benchmark, and compares flag rates between automated systems and human drivers in the same scenarios.

The framing is important: it distinguishes atypicality from harm, treats abstention explicitly, and exposes the limitations of a global human benchmark. However, the statistical evidence does not yet establish the monitor claimed. The conformal construction provides, at most, empirical marginal coverage over a selected and strongly dependent set of moments; it does not validate conditional or sequential decisions, and its severe leave-one-source-out undercoverage contradicts broad transfer claims. More fundamentally, calibration is performed on a model-derived IPV whose construct validity and uncertainty are not established. The consequence and AV–human comparisons remain observational, use overlapping moment-level units, and do not adequately address crossed clustering, selective denominators, confounding, or multiplicity. I therefore regard this as a promising measurement framework whose inferential claims require major reconstruction before it meets the methodological standard of *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

## 2. Major weaknesses

1. **[Location: Sections 2.1, 4.1; Figs. 1–2] — The paper validates discrimination within the inverse-planning model, not the validity of the IPV construct.** The reported “reliability” statistic is the concentration of seven candidate likelihood weights under a fixed trajectory generator, Gaussian error scale, reference geometry and one-second window. Concentration can arise from model rigidity or misspecification and is not evidence that the inferred IPV is accurate. Likewise, agreement between the online and offline estimators (correlation approximately 0.993) is agreement between closely related model outputs, not validation against a known or independently measured preference; the manuscript acknowledges the latter point but nonetheless interprets the output substantively throughout. The human reference range therefore calibrates the distribution of an estimated model output, not a demonstrated latent social preference. At NMI level this distinction is foundational: the monitor may detect departure from its own inverse-planning representation rather than social atypicality. The remedy is a construct-validation programme: parameter-recovery experiments with known simulated preferences; sensitivity to the candidate grid, likelihood scale, window length, map reference and trajectory noise; calibration of the concentration score against estimation error; and external validation against independent behavioural or human-judgement measures. Estimation uncertainty should either be propagated into the reference test or the estimand and all claims should be narrowed explicitly to “atypicality of the inferred IPV under this model.”

2. **[Location: Sections 2.3 and 4.4; Fig. 4] — The conformal claim is not supported at the stated observational unit.** The conformal radius is computed from 455,723 accepted calibration moments and evaluated on 461,937 accepted test moments, although adjacent moments within an interaction share trajectories, overlapping ten-frame windows, context and outcomes. A whole-scene split avoids direct train–test reuse but does not make individual moment scores exchangeable, and pooling all moments in the conformal order statistic overweights long or densely readable interactions. Standard split-conformal finite-sample coverage does not follow for this clustered sequence. The paper partly retreats to empirical marginal coverage, but still repeatedly describes the interval as “calibrated” without uncertainty on the achieved coverage or an effective independent sample size. The analysis should be rebuilt at an exchangeable unit such as scene, run or interaction. Options include cluster/block conformal calibration, a cluster-level maximum or appropriate random within-cluster score, or a clearly justified hierarchical conformal construction. At minimum, report cluster-bootstrap uncertainty for coverage and width, sensitivity to cluster definition and length weighting, and a clean distinction between a formal guarantee and a single-split empirical coverage estimate.

3. **[Location: Sections 2.3, 4.3–4.4; Fig. 4] — Marginal coverage over accepted moments does not support situation-level or online calibration.** The conditional quantile model followed by one global conformal radius targets pointwise marginal coverage over the accepted mixture, which the Methods correctly states. It does not imply 90% coverage conditional on source, geometry, role, risk proxy, interaction phase, support distance or any particular situation. This is especially consequential because the scientific claim is precisely that the range is appropriate “for this situation.” Aggregate coverage can conceal systematic undercoverage in smaller or safety-relevant strata, and no sequential or trajectory-wise guarantee is established. The authors should report prespecified group-conditional and worst-group diagnostics with cluster-aware intervals across the variables used to define context, including intersections and support-distance bins; calibration as a function of interaction time; and scene/trajectory-level simultaneous coverage. If these cannot be established, “calibrated for the situation” should be replaced by the narrower statement that a context-varying interval has empirically near-nominal marginal coverage on one accepted in-domain test mixture.

4. **[Location: Abstract; Sections 2.3, 2.5, 3 and 4.4; Figs. 4 and 6] — The demonstrated source-shift failures are incompatible with the paper’s broad transfer language.** At nominal 90% coverage, leave-one-source-out coverage is 74.3% for Waymo and 75.0% for Lyft, while Argoverse-2 requires 44.3% abstention. These are not modest degradations; they show that neither the fitted interval nor the support gate reliably detects important source shifts. The matched-course human rate of 4.7% outside the range among judgeable moments demonstrates only that alarms are not inflated in that selected sample. It does not establish calibrated conditional coverage, transfer across the target distribution, or robustness under a new source. The claim that the reference “transfers across country, apparatus and task” is therefore too strong. The authors should either implement and independently evaluate a shift-aware calibration procedure, with target-site calibration kept separate from evaluation, or confine the claimed validity to the development source mixture. Coverage, interval width and abstention must be shown jointly for every source and target domain; failure cases should be treated as primary results rather than a boundary mentioned after the headline claim.

5. **[Location: Sections 2.4–2.5 and 4.4–4.6; Figs. 5–6] — Selective denominators prevent a clean comparison of alarm rates.** The monitor judges only 14,099 of 67,861 benchmark candidate moments (20.8%), and the stated reasons show that support selection, not merely readability, is dominant. Yet Fig. 6 compares 9.8% of judgeable AV moments with 4.7% of judgeable human moments without reporting, in the same analysis, the candidate, readable, supported and judged denominators for the human arm by scenario and driver. A selective monitor can change its apparent flag rate by changing whom and when it judges. Consequently, “2.1 times as often” is not interpretable as a population contrast unless judgeability is comparable or the estimand is explicitly limited to each group’s different selected population. Provide complete flow accounting and abstention reasons for natural humans, course humans and AVs; acceptance/flag risk–coverage curves; comparisons on a common-support, scenario-time-matched population; and sensitivity analyses that standardise the context distribution. Report run-level availability, alert onset and duration as well as moment-level rates.

6. **[Location: Sections 2.5 and 4.6; Fig. 6] — The AV–human comparison does not use the correct crossed inferential structure.** The design repeatedly observes 20 human drivers and 19 automated systems across 15 scenario templates. Moments are nested in runs, while runs are crossed by driver/system and scenario. Raw moment counts are not independent replicates, and resampling only cases or runs does not address both shared-agent and shared-scenario dependence. Fig. 6c shows 15 scenario-paired points and “15/15 above parity” but no uncertainty; Fig. 6a also presents the 2.1-fold comparison without a confidence interval. The population represented by 20 licensed course drivers is also not literally the natural-driving population from which the reference was fitted. A crossed mixed-effects, multiway cluster-bootstrap or randomisation-based analysis is required, with scenario and driver/system as inferential units and with the estimand stated (these systems/drivers versus a target population). Report uncertainty for the paired scenario contrast and rate ratio, between-driver/system heterogeneity, missing-run handling (19 × 15 would yield 285 rather than the reported 267 AV runs), and recruitment, order, practice and exclusion details for the human study.

7. **[Location: Sections 2.4, 2.6 and 4.5; Fig. 5 and Fig. ED2] — The “consequence” analysis is highly vulnerable to temporal, mechanical and contextual confounding.** Flags are constructed from the immediately preceding overlapping trajectory window and current relative kinematics, while outcomes start at the same flagged moment and include post-verdict TTC and motion over the next three seconds. Assertive flags may therefore preferentially occur at particular interaction phases or baseline margins that mechanically predict the outcomes. Hundreds of overlapping flagged moments can describe one episode, making the apparent sample much larger and weighting long alert stretches more heavily. Resampling runs handles some variance dependence but does not remove confounding; reassigning whole flag sequences across runs and obtaining an empirical *p* = 0.0199 tests one timing null, not causal attribution. The repeated wording that atypicality “compresses” interaction quality is stronger than the descriptive design permits. A credible analysis should operate on prespecified, non-overlapping alert onsets; compare them with matched within-run or scenario-time controls on pre-verdict TTC, relative kinematics, role, phase and support; show pre-trends and changes from baseline; include system and scenario effects; and report sensitivity to unmeasured confounding. Until then, use explicitly associational language throughout, including titles and figure headings.

8. **[Location: Sections 2.4–2.5, 4.5–4.6; Figs. 5–6] — The confirmatory status and multiple-comparison control are insufficient.** The consequence battery contains distributional summaries, four nested ego-margin thresholds, several counterpart outcomes, three braking thresholds, two atypicality directions and a second human arm. Per-endpoint 95% intervals do not control family-wise or false-discovery error, and describing the battery as frozen does not by itself solve multiplicity. Phrases such as “at every supported threshold” select significance through whether an interval excludes zero, while the nonsignificant 3-s result is treated differently. The human arm further asks whether the same multivariate “signature” recurs but provides no prespecified global similarity statistic. Specify which endpoints are primary and which are exploratory; document when the analysis plan was frozen relative to all data access; apply a hierarchical testing strategy, simultaneous confidence bands or an appropriate multiplicity adjustment; and give estimates and intervals for all advertised quantile changes and cross-arm contrasts. A multivariate/global test of signature replication would be more defensible than counting individually significant endpoints.

9. **[Location: Sections 2.2–2.3 and 4.3; Fig. 3] — The claims that observable context “suffices” and that counterpart preference adds no value are not established.** A case-clustered *p* = 0.86 for an interval-score difference of −0.0002 is a failure to reject a difference, not evidence of equivalence. No equivalence margin, confidence interval, power analysis or independent-source validation is provided. Moreover, leave-one-source-out predictive performance of the full state description is at or below zero in two sources, and the prominent risk-stratified result uses realised post-encroachment time, an outcome jointly determined by behaviour rather than an online pre-behaviour covariate. This makes the “risk-gated” interpretation descriptive and potentially endogenous. Define a practically meaningful equivalence margin for interval score or width, report a cluster-aware confidence interval, and test the ablation on untouched sources. The role/risk claim should be reproduced using prospectively available risk proxies, with explicit acknowledgement that contemporaneous kinematics may already encode counterpart behaviour. Otherwise the conclusion should be “no incremental benefit detected in this analysis,” not sufficiency.

10. **[Location: Abstract; Sections 3 and 4.4–4.6; Algorithms 1–2] — A per-moment retrospective analysis is not yet an evaluated runtime monitor.** The paper uses “online” correctly to mean non-anticipating inputs, but it does not evaluate end-to-end latency, computational failures on vehicle hardware, a sequential alert policy, episode-level false alarms, detection delay, warning duration or time-uniform error. With ten-frame overlapping windows and clustered flags, a 9.8% per-moment outside rate has no direct operational interpretation. The proposed persistence layer is explicitly unevaluated, yet it is the layer a planner or safety operator would consume. The paper should separate an online-computable statistic from a validated runtime monitor. To sustain the stronger claim, evaluate computational latency and throughput, prespecify the sequential aggregation rule, and report run/episode-level operating characteristics under repeated monitoring, including how abstention propagates to the planner interface.

## 3. Minor issues

1. The manuscript does not describe the conditional quantile-regression model, loss, hyperparameter selection, regularisation or tuning split sufficiently to reproduce Fig. 4 from the text. “Released with the code” is not a substitute for methodological specification in the paper.
2. Clarify the exclusion flow from 38,228 corpus interactions to 26,828 interactions in the frozen anchor ledger, and from 4,497,368 anchor rows to the 2,442,625-row human–human reference pool. Provide counts by source and exclusion reason.
3. Report confidence intervals for achieved coverage, interval-width reductions, the 20.9% out-of-fold variance explained, the Fig. 5 median and quartile differences, and every Fig. 6 rate/rate ratio.
4. State the bootstrap interval type, resampling algorithm, number of unique clusters, treatment of clusters with no exposed moments, and random seed. For the placebo *p* = 0.0199, give the number of permutations, test statistic and whether the test was one- or two-sided.
5. Explain why the seven candidates span only 3π/4 while the parameter domain is stated as [−π/2, π/2], and quantify boundary mass or clipping. A reference width expressed as a percentage of the candidate span can appear artificially saturated if the grid truncates plausible values.
6. The 0.20 maximum-weight readability cutoff is close to the uniform value 1/7 and appears ad hoc. Show sensitivity and the distribution of maximum weights; justify why this threshold corresponds to useful discrimination.
7. Figure 2 uses the development split while a confirmatory split remains sealed. Clarify what hypotheses that split is reserved for and whether any central measurability claim has been evaluated outside development data.
8. The Methods should include ethics approval, informed consent, recruitment, driver demographics, compensation, scenario order/randomisation, familiarisation and adverse-event handling for the human reference arm.
9. Clarify the level at which “within frozen situation cells” is implemented in the consequence analysis, provide cell counts and overlap diagnostics, and show covariate balance between flagged and within-range moments.
10. “No engineering failures” on the real-vehicle benchmark should be explicitly distinguished from the 1,826 solver failures in the natural corpus.
11. For Fig. 5d, the plotted fractions are based on acceleration records whereas uncertainty is resampled by run. State whether the estimand is record-weighted or run-weighted and provide a run-level sensitivity analysis.
12. The terms “reference range,” “prediction interval,” “coverage,” “outside rate” and “alarm rate” should be kept distinct. In particular, an outside rate in a shifted population is not itself evidence that conditional predictive coverage transfers.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. What external evidence shows that the inferred IPV orders human behaviour by the intended social-preference construct rather than by fit to the trajectory generator? How often does high candidate-weight concentration coincide with large estimation error in simulations where the generating preference is known?
2. What is the exact exchangeable unit assumed by the conformal argument? If it is the moment, how is that assumption reconciled with overlapping one-second windows and repeated moments per interaction? If it is the scene, why is the conformal quantile computed over moments?
3. How many independent scenes, interactions, logs and drivers contribute to each calibration and test fold, and can any fragments of the same recording trip or physical encounter occur in different folds?
4. What are coverage and interval width by source, role, geometry, risk-proxy bin, interaction phase, support-distance decile and their most important intersections? Which groups account for the Waymo and Lyft leave-one-source-out failures?
5. For course humans and AVs separately, what fractions of all candidate moments fail active-interaction, readability, identity, map/role and human-support gates? Does the 2.1-fold flag-rate ratio persist on an identical common-support set?
6. What inferential population underlies the AV–human contrast: the 19 submitted systems and 20 recruited drivers only, or broader populations of systems and humans? How are repeated runs from the same system/driver and repeated uses of the same scenario represented in the uncertainty calculation?
7. Why are there 267 rather than 285 AV scenario runs, and were any human runs missing from the nominal 300? Were exclusions or failures related to scenario difficulty, system behaviour or judgeability?
8. What variables define the frozen situation cells in the consequence analysis, how many cells contain both flagged and within-range moments, and what is the balance in pre-verdict TTC, distance, relative speed, interaction phase and support score?
9. Are the 472 ego-side flagged moments represented as 472 overlapping exposures, 120 run-level clusters or a smaller number of distinct alert episodes? Do the conclusions persist when only the first onset per run or one onset per non-overlapping episode is analysed?
10. Was the complete endpoint/threshold/direction battery externally timestamped before outcome access? What was the prespecified primary endpoint, and what family of hypotheses was used for error control?
11. What confidence interval supports the assertion that the counterpart-IPV ablation is equivalent to the situation-only model, and what practical equivalence margin was defined before examining the result?
12. What computational latency distribution is achieved at 10 Hz, and how would the per-frame outputs be converted into an actionable warning without invalidating the reported error rates?

## 5. Prioritised revision requests

1. **Rebuild calibration at the independent cluster level.** Define the target unit and estimand; use cluster/block or hierarchical conformal calibration; report cluster-aware uncertainty, group-conditional diagnostics and trajectory-level operating characteristics.
2. **Resolve the distribution-shift contradiction.** Either add a genuinely held-out, shift-aware calibration/validation protocol or narrow all transfer and deployment claims to the source mixture for which empirical marginal coverage is demonstrated.
3. **Establish what IPV measures.** Add independent construct validation and sensitivity/parameter-recovery analyses, calibrate the readability statistic against estimation error, and propagate or explicitly bound estimator uncertainty.
4. **Reanalyse the human–AV comparison with selective denominators and crossed clustering.** Supply complete abstention flows, a common-support comparison, multiway uncertainty over scenario and driver/system, missing-run accounting and human-study details.
5. **Recast and reanalyse the consequence study as an observational event analysis.** Use non-overlapping alert onsets, matched within-scenario/time controls, pre-trend and baseline adjustment, crossed clustering and causal-language discipline.
6. **Implement a prespecified multiplicity strategy.** Identify primary endpoints, provide simultaneous or adjusted intervals for the frozen battery, and use a global test for cross-arm signature replication.
7. **Align the runtime claim with the evidence.** Either evaluate latency and a sequential alert rule with episode-level error/delay metrics or describe the contribution as an online-computable retrospective monitor prototype.

- **Assuming a competent major revision:** 45%. This would require substantial reanalysis and claim narrowing; the probability would be lower if independent IPV construct validation or cluster-valid calibration cannot be supplied.
