## C1 — the round-6 self-contradiction was repaired in the caption and left standing in the body — **TRUE**

Round 6 recorded finding F3: the paper both asserted that a global reference range at the strictest
level "can never flag anything" and reported that the same range over-covers by 2.8 points (i.e.
achieves 97.8% coverage, so 2.2% of readings fall outside it and are flagged). The round-6 log
records F3 as repaired.

The repair reached only the figure caption. Both statements are still in the manuscript:

- Results body: "it stays informative at the strictest level, where the global range spans
  essentially the whole admissible scale **and can never flag a moment**."
- The caption of the same figure: "at the 95% level its width equals 100.00% of the admissible span,
  so it is as wide as the quantity itself and retains almost no power to discriminate --- **it still
  flags**, but only where the reading sits at the very edge of what the estimator can express, and
  it pays for that width by over-covering."

"Can never flag a moment" and "it still flags" are directly opposed, in the same paper, about the
same object. The caption's version is the correct one and is consistent with the 2.8-point
over-coverage also printed in the caption; the body's version is the residue of the pre-repair
wording.

A third statement, in Methods, is unobjectionable because it makes only the width claim and draws
no consequence from it: "the global 95% range spans essentially the whole admissible scale."

**Disposition: fix the body clause after the round closes.** The argument does not depend on the
absolute claim — conditioning is justified by the width reduction and the coverage cost, both of
which are reported — so the repair is a deletion, not a retreat.

**Process note.** This is the second time a round-6 item has been recorded as repaired when only one
of its occurrences was edited. When a claim appears in body, caption and Methods, repairing it means
grepping for every occurrence, not editing the one the referee quoted.
