# Reviewer B report

## 1. Summary assessment

This manuscript proposes a frame-level monitor that estimates an interaction preference value (IPV), abstains when that estimate or its human reference is deemed unreadable, and flags values outside a situation-conditioned human interval. It reports near-nominal marginal coverage on naturalistic data, associations between assertive-side flags and tighter subsequent interactions on a staged real-vehicle benchmark, and a matched human-driver audit. The empirical question is potentially important, but the paper does not yet establish a runtime-verification or runtime-assurance result. The monitor is not specified sufficiently to reproduce its semantics; the claimed calibration exceeds what the stated split-conformal construction guarantees under temporally dependent, selectively accepted, shifted data; and “online” is not supported by end-to-end timing, causal-input, fault, or assurance-architecture evidence. The consequence analysis also does not demonstrate information beyond current kinematics or established safety monitors, because alerts and outcomes are temporally overlapping, highly clustered, and built from related trajectory signals. Finally, the human reference arm lacks the methodological, ethical and inferential detail needed to support the transfer claims. These are central evidence gaps requiring new analysis and experiments, not editorial clarification. I therefore do not consider the principal claims established at the standard of *Nature Machine Intelligence*.

## 2. Major weaknesses

1. **[Location: Methods 4.1–4.4; Eqs. 1–3; Algorithms 1–2] — The monitor has no reproducible operational semantics.** Equation 1 leaves the candidate-trajectory generator and probability model essentially undefined: the planning objective, agent dynamics, coordinate frame, candidate horizon, treatment of map uncertainty, normalization of trajectory error and meaning of the fixed likelihood scale \(\sigma=0.1\) are absent. Likewise, the interaction-opportunity rule, “trajectory/reference information” score, counterpart-identity stability score, support test, out-of-distribution test, causal risk proxies, quantile-regression model, hyperparameters and nearly all gate thresholds are described only by names. A seven-point candidate grid followed by Gaussian MSE weighting is not self-explanatory as a preference estimator. Consequently, independent readers cannot determine which input traces produce a verdict, reproduce the abstention set, or audit whether the monitor uses information unavailable at decision time. At NMI level, a monitor claimed to be auditable must be specified as an executable mathematical object, not deferred to future code release. The remedy is a complete formal input/output contract, explicit feature and timing table, equations or pseudocode for every gate and reason code, the candidate planner and likelihood in full, all frozen thresholds and split assignments, and a reproducibility package available during review. A trace-level conformance test suite should give expected outputs for normal, boundary and failure cases.

2. **[Location: Results 2.3; Fig. 4; Methods 4.4; Algorithm 1] — The conformal claims do not follow from the stated construction.** Split conformal provides a finite-sample marginal guarantee under exchangeability for the calibration/test unit actually used. Here, calibration retains at most one score per case at four progress anchors, whereas Fig. 4 reports hundreds of thousands of evaluated moments; moments within a case are strongly dependent; gates select accepted windows; and the paper then discusses transfer across source, country, apparatus and task. None of pointwise moment-level coverage, conditional coverage by situation, sequential alarm validity, trajectory-wise coverage, or validity under this distribution shift is guaranteed by the stated procedure. Freezing the gate does not restore exchangeability, and calibration conditional on acceptance does not characterize the combined monitor’s behavior if acceptance changes under shift. The manuscript acknowledges some boundaries but still calls the resulting monitor “calibrated” and uses nominal outside rates as an audit anchor. This is especially problematic because Fig. 3c reports zero or negative held-out-source \(R^2\), directly warning that the state-to-reading relation does not transport. The remedy is to define the conformal unit and estimand unambiguously; provide scene-grouped train/guard/calibration/test counts and source allocations; report case-balanced, trajectory-wise and sequential coverage with cluster uncertainty; audit coverage and abstention jointly by source, geometry, risk and progress; and either use a dependence/shift-aware construction with justified assumptions or restrict the claim to marginal coverage on exchangeable in-domain cases. The sequential persistence rule also needs its own calibration target and test-set results.

3. **[Location: Results 2.1; Fig. 2; Methods 4.1–4.2] — IPV “readability” and estimator validity are not established.** The normalized candidate weights arise from a deliberately misspecified, coarse seven-candidate model; they are not shown to be calibrated posterior probabilities or uncertainty estimates. The maximum-weight threshold of 0.20 is only slightly above the uniform value of \(1/7\), and neither this threshold nor the fixed noise scale is validated. Fig. 2a shows that the chosen concentration statistic differs under interaction and control pairings, but this does not establish that the selected IPV is identifiable, accurate, or semantically a preference. Fig. 2b is more concerning: the supposedly most readable estimates move by about 0.30 rad per frame, nearly twice the less-readable estimates, which is compatible with instability as well as information. Agreement with an offline estimate generated by the same model and lane reference is not external validation. Model mismatch, reference-geometry artifacts, perception noise or a restricted candidate set could all be converted into apparent social atypicality. The remedy is controlled validation with known ground-truth preferences or planted reward weights, plus independent behavioral or human-judgment validation; calibration curves linking the proposed reliability to estimation error; sensitivity to candidate density, likelihood scale, window length, planner family, map error, localization noise and counterpart assignment; and comparison with alternative inverse-planning estimators. Until then, “readable” should be described as “candidate-discriminative under this model,” not as a validated measurement property.

4. **[Location: Introduction; Results 2.6; Discussion; Methods “Planner interface”; Algorithm 2] — The work does not yet demonstrate an online runtime-assurance component.** All reported evidence appears to come from replayed trajectories. There is no end-to-end implementation latency, worst-case execution time, memory load, deadline-miss behavior, sensor timestamp discipline, causal replay audit, or experiment showing operation at the claimed 10 Hz. The monitor assumes stable map/role/counterpart information but does not evaluate perception faults, delayed or missing tracks, map errors, counterpart switches, multi-agent ambiguity or distribution-shift detection. Abstention is treated rhetorically as responsible behavior, yet an assurance architecture must specify what system guarantee remains during abstention and whether abstentions concentrate in the hardest encounters. The planner-facing actions are only a proposed mapping; no arbitration, fallback safety, chattering/hysteresis, controllability, or closed-loop effect is evaluated. This prevents comparison with runtime assurance, STL monitoring, reachability, barrier-function shields or RSS: unlike those methods, the present output has neither a formal property semantics nor an associated guaranteed safe response. The remedy is an end-to-end causal implementation on representative hardware, with latency distributions and worst-case bounds; a fault-injection and coverage study; an explicit assume–guarantee contract for inputs, verdicts, abstentions and degraded operation; and a closed-loop integration showing how persistent warnings alter planning without violating a separately verified safety envelope. Otherwise, the paper should be positioned as offline evaluation of a prospective online indicator, not a deployed runtime monitor.

5. **[Location: Results 2.4 and 2.6; Fig. 5; Methods 4.5; Fig. ED2] — The consequence analysis does not establish prospective, incremental information.** Flags are assigned per moment from recent trajectory windows and current kinematics, while “subsequent” margins, speed reductions and speed ranges are measured over nearby or overlapping windows from the same interaction. The manuscript does not provide exact temporal separation between estimator inputs, persistent-alert onset, baseline covariates and each outcome window. Current relative kinematics and “causal risk proxies” can therefore drive both the flag and the alleged consequence. Repeated overlapping moments from 175 scenarios are then presented as \(n=472\) versus \(11{,}669\); scenario bootstrap intervals do not by themselves make a moment-weighted contrast an unbiased episode-level estimand. The finding that emergency thresholds are less frequent among flagged moments does not show added value beyond collision safety; it may simply show that the social flag occurs preferentially in lower-risk states. No RSS, STL, time-to-collision/post-encroachment, reachability-margin, surprise or learned-anomaly baseline is tested for incremental prediction. Fig. ED2 is explicitly selected from only two runs meeting consequence-shaped display criteria and cannot resolve this concern. The remedy is a preregistered first-alert-per-run or non-overlapping episode analysis with a strict prediction horizon; hierarchical inference at driver/system/run/scenario levels; adjustment or matching for pre-alert risk and kinematics with overlap diagnostics; negative-control outcomes; and head-to-head incremental evaluation against established safety and behavior monitors. Claims should remain associational unless an intervention or defensible causal design is added.

6. **[Location: Results 2.5; Fig. 6; Methods 4.6] — The human reference audit is under-described and statistically insufficient for the stated transfer claim.** The study comprises 20 drivers × 15 scripted scenarios, but the manuscript gives no recruitment criteria beyond licensing, demographics, driving experience, country/cultural background, apparatus details, training, scenario order, randomization/counterbalancing, repeated-exposure handling, exclusions, missing runs, safety protocol, ethics approval or consent. Panel 6a provides no uncertainty, and panel 6c reports 15/15 points above parity without a paired model that accounts for repeated observations within driver, scenario and automated system. A lower human flag rate than the nominal 10% is not evidence of transfer without a prespecified equivalence/non-inferiority margin: it may reflect over-coverage, narrower behavior on a closed course, adaptation to repeated scripted interactions or differential abstention. The “same consequence signature” also requires effect estimates and uncertainty for the human arm, not visual similarity of a small set of ratios. The remedy is complete human-subject and apparatus reporting; ethics and consent statements; a flow diagram from 300 planned cells to analyzed runs; driver- and scenario-level abstention/flag distributions; multilevel paired inference with confidence intervals; prespecified equivalence testing against the held-out natural-driving rate; and sensitivity to order, learning and driver heterogeneity. Transfer should be limited to these 15 staged scenarios unless replicated on held-out scenarios and drivers.

7. **[Location: Title, Abstract, Introduction, Results 2.5–2.6 and Discussion] — “Social compliance” remains a normative overclaim relative to the evidence.** The measured reference population is observed human driving, not demonstrated competent, acceptable, legal or preferred behavior. The manuscript repeatedly and correctly states that atypicality is not inappropriateness, but the title and framing nevertheless present a monitor of “socially compliant autonomous driving.” The consequence results show compression of routine margins and speed behavior, while explicitly failing to establish harm; the human study excludes preference ratings and harm labels. Thus, the current instrument detects model-relative deviation from a selected human distribution, not social compliance. This distinction is decisive for broad machine-intelligence significance and safety-case use: a human-typical act can be unsafe or undesirable, and a beneficial machine behavior can be atypical. The remedy is either to rename and consistently frame the contribution as online conditional human-atypicality monitoring, or to add independent normative validation using blinded human judgments, behavioral preference, interaction burden and safety-relevant outcomes across cultures and road-user classes. Any future assurance claim must keep empirical typicality separate from legal/safety specifications and from stakeholder acceptability.

8. **[Location: Results 2.3; Fig. 4; Methods 4.3; Data and Code Availability] — Central quantitative and reproducibility claims are internally inconsistent or unavailable.** The text states a reference-width reduction “above 40%,” whereas Fig. 4 reports reductions of 21%, 20% and 8% at 80%, 90% and 95% coverage; at 95% the conditioned interval still spans 92% of the admissible IPV range, which is not operationally sharp. The manuscript also states that transfer to an unseen data source is not established, yet later describes transfer across country, apparatus and task in broad terms. Processed time series, verdicts and code are promised only after publication, preventing verification of these high-dimensional, selection-sensitive analyses during review. The remedy is to reconcile every width definition and denominator, report absolute and relative widths with uncertainty, quantify operational precision/false-alert trade-offs, make the frozen analysis artifacts available for peer review, and constrain transfer language to the exact populations and scenario support tested.

## 3. Minor issues

1. **[Methods 4.1]** Define whether MSE is in metres, squared metres, normalized coordinates or a dimensionless trajectory space; otherwise \(\sigma=0.1\) is uninterpretable.
2. **[Methods 4.2]** “The event-level summary” is fixed as a mean despite Fig. 2c showing that summary choice changes signs in up to 22% of episodes. Explain why the mean was selected and whether this decision preceded outcome analysis.
3. **[Fig. 1b]** The flow-chart arrows do not cleanly encode the three mutually exclusive outputs. The “no” branch from mechanism 1 should lead only to abstention; the mechanism-2 branches should explicitly distinguish below, inside and above range.
4. **[Figs. 1, ED1 and ED2]** Use consistent language for “above/below range” and “more assertive/over-yielding.” Readers should not have to infer sign conventions separately in each panel.
5. **[Fig. 2a]** Name and define the plotted statistic mathematically; “change in how sharply the reading is identified” is not a reproducible axis label.
6. **[Fig. 2]** Give numerical confidence intervals and clustering units for every point, not only sample counts.
7. **[Fig. 3 and Results 2.2]** Explain the small count differences between the text and panel b (for example, Waymo 23,218 versus 23,211 and analogous differences for the other sources).
8. **[Fig. 4]** Report uncertainty on width, coverage and explained-variance estimates and show results separately by source rather than only pooled over moments.
9. **[Fig. 5]** The caption describes blue marks, whereas the supplied figure uses teal for the within-range group. More importantly, define “ego margin to counterpart” and justify each emergency threshold independently of the observed results.
10. **[Fig. 5]** State how multiple thresholds and consequence endpoints were handled statistically; bootstrap confidence intervals alone do not address multiplicity.
11. **[Fig. 6]** Report numerator/denominator counts at every nominal level and uncertainty for panels a and c; avoid displaying three decimal places for the AV flag rate when the human rate is shown to one decimal place.
12. **[Algorithm 2]** `Counterpart-Unreadable` is listed as an abstention reason in Methods 4.4 but the algorithm gates only ego readability before the generic support checks. Reconcile the logic.
13. **[Methods 4.5]** Correct “the the onset” and define “first persistent deviation,” including persistence length and whether it was frozen before inspection of consequences.
14. **[Methods note on scope]** `claims_register.md` is not part of the manuscript and should not be invoked as evidence unless supplied as peer-review material.
15. **[Reference 53]** Remove the editorial placeholder “Verify the specific challenge edition/year for the data used” and provide the exact benchmark edition, version, access date and data subset.
16. **[Data/Code Availability]** Provide stable repository/accession information, licenses and a review-access mechanism rather than only promises of release after publication.
17. **[Human arm]** State whether any driver, scenario or run contributed zero judgeable moments and how such cells entered the paired comparison.
18. **[Scope]** The introduction includes cyclists and pedestrians, but all demonstrated interactions appear vehicle–vehicle. Restrict the scope or test additional road-user classes.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. What exact optimization problem and dynamics generate each candidate trajectory in Eq. 1, and can every input be proven to be timestamp-available before the emitted verdict?
2. What is the empirical relationship between the maximum normalized candidate weight and IPV estimation error? Why is 0.20 the readability threshold, and how often would the selected candidate change under a denser grid or another likelihood scale?
3. What are the exact scene counts and source allocations in train, guard, calibration and test sets? Were all frames, cases and related scenes from a physical encounter kept in one split?
4. What exchangeability claim is being made for the conformal procedure: moment, case-anchor, trajectory, scene or source? Which reported coverage quantities have a finite-sample guarantee under that claim?
5. How do accepted-window coverage, total active-interaction coverage and abstention-reason distributions change separately for each naturalistic source and for the human and AV benchmark arms?
6. Was the complete pipeline ever executed online at 10 Hz on vehicle-representative hardware? If so, what are median, 99th-percentile and worst-case latencies, and what happens after a missed deadline or stale input?
7. Do the trajectory samples used to estimate IPV or trigger persistence overlap temporally with the windows used to compute post-verdict speed and margin outcomes? Please provide a timing diagram with exact indices.
8. After conditioning on pre-alert relative speed, distance, time-to-conflict, role, scenario and prior safety-monitor outputs, what incremental information does the IPV flag retain?
9. How does this monitor compare on identical traces with RSS/STL or reachability-based safety margins, simple kinematic thresholding, and a learned trajectory-anomaly baseline in detection rate, lead time, false-alert burden and abstention?
10. Why does the situation model have zero or negative held-out-source \(R^2\) in Fig. 3c while the manuscript later claims transfer of the reference? What component transfers: the conditional mean, interval coverage, flag rate, consequence association, or only lack of alarm inflation in the 15 staged scenarios?
11. What prespecified statistical hypothesis supports the claim that the human arm has “no alarm inflation”? Was equivalence or non-inferiority tested, and what margin was chosen before data access?
12. Please provide the human-study ethics approval, consent process, participant characteristics, scenario ordering, training/repeat exposure, exclusions and adverse-event information.
13. How many independent automated systems, runs per system and human runs contribute to each consequence analysis? Were systems or drivers included as resampling levels in addition to scenario?
14. What guarantee or operational behavior is intended when the monitor abstains during a high-risk interaction? Is a separate safety monitor always authoritative, and can the social monitor ever inhibit its intervention?
15. Which claims would remain if independent human raters judged flagged and matched unflagged clips as equally acceptable? The current manuscript should specify this falsification condition.

## 5. Prioritised revision requests

1. **Specify and release an independently executable monitor.** Provide complete estimator, feature, gate, support/OOD, timing and verdict semantics; all frozen thresholds and splits; review-access code/data; and trace-level conformance tests.
2. **Rebuild the calibration analysis around the actual dependence and selection structure.** State the exchangeability unit, use scene/case-grouped splits, report joint coverage–abstention behavior, sequence-level validity and source/state-stratified uncertainty, and remove guarantees not justified under shift.
3. **Validate the IPV estimator and reliability gate.** Add ground-truth or planted-preference experiments, independent behavioral validation, error calibration and sensitivity to planner, grid, likelihood, window, perception and map misspecification.
4. **Demonstrate genuine online operation and an assurance contract.** Report end-to-end real-time performance and causal input availability; test faults and deadline misses; define the relationship to an authoritative safety monitor and the system response during warnings and abstentions.
5. **Reanalyse consequences as prospective, clustered episodes and establish incremental value.** Use non-overlapping first alerts, fixed horizons, hierarchical inference, baseline-risk adjustment and direct comparison with RSS/STL/reachability, kinematic and anomaly-detection baselines.
6. **Complete and properly analyse the human reference arm.** Add ethics and full protocol reporting, participant and run flow, multilevel paired estimates with confidence intervals, prespecified equivalence tests, abstention analysis and sensitivity to order/learning/heterogeneity.
7. **Align the claim with the construct.** Either rename the method as conditional human-atypicality monitoring or add independent normative and harm validation sufficient to support “social compliance.”
8. **Resolve quantitative contradictions and reproducibility gaps.** In particular, reconcile the >40% narrowing claim with Fig. 4, constrain transfer statements, fix benchmark provenance, and provide stable data/code access.

## 6. Acceptance probability

- **As submitted:** 5%.
- **Assuming a competent major revision:** 40%. This estimate assumes the revision includes new estimator validation, statistically valid sequence/transfer analyses, an online implementation study, and a fully reported human experiment; prose-only revision would not materially change the probability.

## 7. Recommendation

**Reject.** The central idea could motivate a substantially stronger resubmission, but the current paper lacks the monitor semantics, validity conditions, runtime evidence and independent validation required for its principal claims. The necessary remedies amount to new methodological and experimental work rather than a conventional revision.
