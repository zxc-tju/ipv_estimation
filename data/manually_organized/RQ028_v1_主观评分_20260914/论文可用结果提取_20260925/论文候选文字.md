## Results candidate

### Subjective ratings distinguish atypicality from the experience of interaction

We examined whether departures from the human reference were also reflected in human ratings of the selected interaction segments. The rating dataset comprised 40 participants in 20 pairs, with 1,192 position-specific ratings from 596 completed trials across 90 segments. From the counterpart position, perceived atypicality was higher for both assertive-side and accommodating-side segments than for within-range segments: the mean within-participant differences were +0.639 [+0.318, +0.992] and +0.682 [+0.548, +0.817] rating points, respectively (95% pair-bootstrap intervals on the 1–7 scale). This links the supplied monitoring categories to perceived atypicality in the evaluated stimulus set.

The two sides were distinguished more clearly by comfort and reported interaction cost. Relative to within-range segments, assertive-side segments received lower counterpart-position comfort ratings (-1.091 [-1.226, -0.961] points) and higher interaction-cost ratings (+2.070 [+1.885, +2.263] points). Direct comparison of the two outside-range categories gave a comfort difference of -0.870 [-0.974, -0.766] points and an interaction-cost difference of +1.163 [+1.019, +1.300] points for the assertive side. The comfort contrast was also present from the ego position, and the directions of the principal comfort and cost contrasts were retained when comparisons were restricted to shared scenarios.

Accommodating-side segments also received slightly lower counterpart-position comfort ratings than within-range segments (-0.221 [-0.351, -0.100] points), together with higher interaction-cost ratings (+0.908 [+0.771, +1.051] points). These subjective endpoints complement the vehicle-motion consequences examined separately. They describe differences in perceived interaction experience; they do not by themselves establish behavioural inappropriateness, actual harm or a causal effect of crossing the reference boundary.

## Statistical-analysis text supported by this extraction

This exploratory secondary analysis retained all 596 trials included in the supplied final rating cohort. Four aborted trials contributed no completed questionnaires and were excluded from these rating summaries. Ratings were analysed separately by position. For each participant, ratings were averaged within category and differences between categories were calculated within participant. Estimates gave equal weight to participants. To preserve dependence between paired participants, percentile confidence intervals were obtained from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of the 20 participant pairs. These intervals condition on the selected stimulus set. Two-sided one-sample t-tests used the 20 pair-averaged differences (19 degrees of freedom); Holm correction was applied across all 30 combinations of available item, position and category contrast. Reported 95% intervals are pointwise, not simultaneous intervals. In a separate scenario-composition sensitivity analysis, ratings were first averaged within segment, then within scenario and category, and differences were averaged over scenarios containing both categories. This analysis used 8, 9 and 12 common scenarios for assertive-minus-within, accommodating-minus-within and assertive-minus-accommodating contrasts, respectively. Scenario intervals resampled common scenarios and condition on the observed raters; they are not joint participant-and-stimulus intervals. All analyses used Python 3.9.6, NumPy 2.0.2, pandas 2.3.3 and SciPy 1.13.1, with random seed 20260925.
