# Review of “Online monitoring of socially compliant autonomous driving”

## 1. Summary assessment

This manuscript proposes a three-stage monitor: estimate an interaction preference value (IPV) from a rolling trajectory window, abstain when the estimate or situation is judged unreadable, and compare readable estimates with a situation-conditioned human reference interval. It applies the framework to four naturalistic driving datasets and a matched-scenario real-vehicle benchmark, reporting calibrated marginal coverage, narrower conditional intervals, more frequent assertive-side flags for automated vehicles than for matched human drivers, and associations between flags and reduced interaction margins or stronger counterpart speed responses.

The framing is potentially important: online social monitoring should indeed distinguish an atypical reading from an unavailable one and should condition any human reference on context. However, the empirical evidence currently establishes a detector of model-defined trajectory atypicality, not a monitor of social compliance. IPV lacks independent construct validation; the consequence analyses are vulnerable to temporal overlap, mathematical coupling and state confounding; conformal validity under clustered multi-source data is insufficiently demonstrated; and the human arm does not establish transfer or calibration. Several essential methodological details and uncertainty estimates are also absent. These problems affect the central interpretation rather than presentation alone. I therefore do not consider the main claims established at NMI level.

## 2. Major weaknesses

1. **[Location: Abstract; Introduction; Sections 2.1 and 4.1; Figs. 1–2] — The monitored construct is not independently validated as social preference.** IPV is inferred from the same observed trajectories that the paper subsequently interprets as assertive, accommodating and socially atypical. Agreement between a causal online implementation and an offline implementation of the same estimator (correlation 0.993) tests implementation consistency, not construct validity. Sharper candidate likelihoods during “real interactions” likewise show that the inverse model discriminates its seven candidate trajectories in those windows; they do not show that the recovered angle corresponds to a human social preference, courtesy, comprehensibility or acceptability. The candidate generator, utility/cost functions and behavioural assumptions that give the IPV its semantics are not specified sufficiently here, and no sensitivity is shown to those assumptions, the seven-point grid, the likelihood scale or alternative forward models. This is decisive because all later labels and reference ranges inherit the meaning of this latent variable. The authors should validate IPV against evidence external to the fitted trajectory model: for example, controlled incentive-compatible interaction choices, independently collected human judgements of assertiveness/acceptability, or interventions that manipulate social trade-offs while holding geometry and risk fixed. Convergent and discriminant validity against SVO-based, courtesy, game-theoretic and simpler kinematic constructs are needed, together with sensitivity to the forward-model specification and candidate grid.

2. **[Location: Sections 2.4, 4.1 and 4.5; Fig. 5 and Fig. ED2] — The claimed consequence signature may be mechanically or temporally coupled to the alert.** The online estimate uses the most recent one second of ego and counterpart trajectories, while the reported consequences include ego margin, counterpart speed reduction and speed variation “from the verdict” or “over the same window.” The manuscript does not establish a strictly non-overlapping exposure and outcome design. Fig. ED2 is especially concerning: counterpart deceleration is already underway around the selected alert stretch, so the monitor may be labelling an interaction response that has begun rather than prospectively identifying a state that predicts a later response. Because counterpart motion also enters the inverse-planning likelihood, the apparent two-sided convergence can arise by construction. Overlapping rolling windows and repeated flagged moments from the same run further amplify this dependence. “Followed by” is therefore not justified by the current description, even as a descriptive temporal claim. The remedy is a pre-specified landmark analysis: compute one alert from data available only up to time (t_0), impose a washout interval longer than the estimation window, and measure outcomes only after that interval. Report first-alert-per-run and non-overlapping-event analyses, pre-trend and lead/lag plots, placebo alert times, exclusion of cases with braking already underway, and sensitivity to horizon and persistence definitions. Inference must cluster at least by run/system/scenario as dictated by the sampling design.

3. **[Location: Sections 2.4 and 2.6; Fig. 5] — There is no evidence that IPV alerts add information beyond state and ordinary kinematic anomaly measures.** Flagged and within-range moments are not exchangeable. The flag is a function of trajectory behaviour and a context-conditioned interval, while the reported outcomes are also functions of trajectory geometry and kinematics. Scenario and “initial risk” adjustment is asserted but no estimand, model, covariates, balance diagnostic or adjusted effect is shown. The observation that emergency-threshold rates do not increase is not a comparison against collision-safety or interaction-risk monitors and does not establish incremental value. A low-risk but assertive state could simultaneously produce an IPV deviation, a smaller comfortable margin and fewer emergencies. The authors should compare prospective performance with strong, transparent baselines: current and trend-adjusted TTC/PET or margin, relative speed/acceleration/jerk, trajectory prediction residual or likelihood, a learned context-conditioned behavioural anomaly score, and relevant surprise/expectation monitors. They should match or weight flagged and control moments on the complete observable state and pre-alert trajectories, demonstrate covariate balance, and report incremental prediction or association (with uncertainty) beyond those baselines. Without this analysis, the paper has not shown what the social monitor contributes beyond a repackaging of the inputs.

4. **[Location: Sections 2.2–2.3 and 4.3–4.4; Figs. 3–4] — The calibration claim is not supported under the actual clustered, multi-source deployment problem.** Split conformal prediction guarantees marginal coverage under an appropriate exchangeability regime, but the data contain dense temporal observations nested within interactions, scenes and sources. The manuscript says that at most one score per case per progress anchor is used for calibration, yet Fig. 4 reports 461,937 evaluated moments and does not show whether test uncertainty or coverage inference respects case and scene clustering. The exact scene-level split, overlap prevention, source composition of each partition, quantile model, hyperparameters, support rule and all frozen thresholds are missing. More importantly, Fig. 3c shows leave-one-source-out prediction at or below zero (R^2) for two sources and near zero for the others, while the deployed benchmark is another substantial domain shift. In-distribution marginal coverage cannot establish conditional or cross-source validity. The authors should provide an auditable split table and leakage analysis; evaluate coverage, width, abstention and flag rate by source, geometry, risk, progress and other pre-specified groups; use cluster-aware calibration/inference or justify why case-level sampling is sufficient; and perform a genuinely source-held-out calibration/validation study. The text must separate in-distribution marginal validity from group-conditional and shifted-source performance.

5. **[Location: Sections 2.5 and 4.6; Fig. 6] — The matched human arm does not demonstrate that the reference “transfers across country, apparatus and task.”** A 4.7% outside rate under a nominal 90% interval is lower than both the nominal 10% rate and the 9.72% held-out natural-driving rate. This shows non-inflation in this sample, but not calibration, equivalence, or preservation of sensitivity; it may equally indicate a conservative interval, altered judgeability, or restricted behaviour on the test course. The 20 drivers are not “the population that defined the reference,” which was assembled from different naturalistic datasets and populations. The 2.1-fold AV–human contrast is reported from moment denominators without a confidence interval or a hierarchical test accounting for repeated observations within 20 drivers, automated systems, runs and 15 scenarios. “15/15 scenarios above parity” also needs a paired effect distribution and appropriate unit-level inference, not only a sign count. The paper must report recruitment, demographics and driving experience, country/context, instructions, familiarisation, order/randomisation, safety protocol, exclusions, ethics approval and consent; judgeability and abstention by arm and reason; uncertainty/equivalence margins for transfer; and a driver-by-scenario/system-by-scenario hierarchical analysis. Claims should be restricted to the sampled drivers and scenarios unless broader evidence is supplied.

6. **[Location: Title; Abstract; Introduction; Discussion] — “Social compliance” and “interaction quality” remain normative claims unsupported by the endpoints.** The manuscript explicitly states that atypicality is not inappropriateness and that harm prediction is not established, which is an appropriate boundary. Nevertheless, the title, contribution claims and repeated language of social compliance, human norms and interaction quality invite precisely that normative interpretation. Atypical assertive-side moments are reportedly associated with fewer, not more, emergency events, and no human preference, perceived comfort, predictability, trust, burden or acceptability outcome is measured. Stronger counterpart speed response is not inherently undesirable; it may be a normal and efficient resolution of a low-risk interaction. To retain the social-compliance claim, the authors need an independent human-centred validation showing that alerts identify behaviour judged less acceptable, less predictable or more burdensome under controlled context. Otherwise the paper should be reframed throughout as monitoring **conditional human-trajectory atypicality**, with “interaction quality,” “socially compliant,” “normative” and planner-action claims removed or sharply qualified.

7. **[Location: Sections 2.1, 2.3, 4.1–4.4 and Algorithms 1–2] — The proposed runtime monitor is not reproducible or operationally validated.** Essential definitions are deferred or absent: the candidate-trajectory generation and cost model; the exact reliability statistic (u_a); (q_a) and (c_a); all thresholds (u_0,q_0,c_0); interaction-opportunity horizon and closing-geometry rules; quantile-regression model and features; human-support and out-of-distribution gates; persistence calibration; and reason-code frequencies. A seven-candidate inverse-planning loop may or may not be feasible online, but no runtime, hardware, throughput, latency or end-to-end causal-availability test is reported. The method assumes a stable principal counterpart, route-conditioned map reference and pairwise interaction, yet robustness to map/role error, identity switches, multiple simultaneous counterparts and non-vehicle road users is not tested. A complete frozen specification, computational benchmark and failure-mode evaluation are required before “runtime,” “online” and “planner-agnostic” deployment claims are credible.

8. **[Location: Sections 2.3 and 4.3; Fig. 4; Methods and reference 53] — Internal inconsistencies and incomplete reporting prevent verification of key quantitative claims.** Section 2.3 says conditioning reduces interval width by “more than 40%,” and Section 4.3 again says “above 40%,” whereas Fig. 4a reports reductions of 21%, 20% and 8% at the 80%, 90% and 95% levels. This is not a minor rounding issue. The Methods refer to an unavailable internal `claims_register.md`; reference 53 still contains the instruction “Verify the specific challenge edition/year for the data used”; the confirmatory split is said to remain sealed in Fig. 2 but no later unsealing or confirmatory result is identified; and the consequence protocol states that it controls for scenario and initial risk without presenting the model or adjusted estimates. The authors should conduct a full numerical and claim audit, distinguish development from confirmatory results in every table/figure, identify all analysis populations and exclusions with a flow diagram, report complete statistical models and multiplicity handling, and replace internal placeholders with citable, versioned information.

9. **[Location: Introduction; Sections 2.2, 2.6 and Discussion] — Novelty and generality are asserted without an adequate empirical comparison to adjacent methods.** The paper distinguishes itself verbally from SVO-based planning, courteous planning, learned behavioural anomaly detection, expectation/surprise monitoring, social-navigation evaluation and competent-human reference models, but it does not compare outputs or failure cases with any of them. The evidence is limited to pairwise vehicle interactions at mapped conflict points and 15 staged scenarios; it does not support statements extending to cyclists, pedestrians, general social navigation, or autonomous agents beyond driving. A convincing NMI contribution requires either empirical head-to-head comparison showing a distinct and useful capability or a narrower novelty claim. Scope should be explicitly limited to the tested pairwise vehicle-interaction regime, with multi-agent and vulnerable-road-user settings treated as future work.

## 3. Minor issues

1. Equation 2 uses “an readability indicator”; all gate variables, units, numerical thresholds and directionality should be defined at first use.
2. Fig. 2a does not define the plotted “change in how sharply the reading is identified,” its reference value, or its units. The construction of each control and whether controls are paired should be explicit.
3. Fig. 2b states “uncertainty not shown” and gives no sample sizes. This is a claimed empirical distinction and requires clustered uncertainty and an effect estimate, not two point pairs.
4. Fig. 2c should give the number of episodes, uncertainty for medians and all three pairwise sign-flip rates rather than the range 7–22% alone.
5. Fig. 3c needs confidence intervals and an explicit baseline definition for (R^2). Negative held-out values should be discussed as failed transport rather than visually tethered to zero.
6. Fig. 4 should show distributions of interval width rather than only means, and report accepted-case counts and abstention reasons at each nominal level.
7. In Fig. 5, the unit labelled (n) is a moment but uncertainty is bootstrapped over 175 scenarios. The figure should also report unique runs, systems and alerts, and show uncertainty bands for the empirical distributions.
8. The four emergency thresholds in Fig. 5b and three braking thresholds in Fig. 5d create a multiple-testing issue. State whether these were pre-specified and report multiplicity-adjusted or simultaneous intervals.
9. Median ratios in Fig. 5c can be unstable when the within-range median is near zero. Report the underlying values and absolute differences alongside ratios.
10. Fig. 6a and c explicitly omit uncertainty. These panels carry the principal human–AV comparison and require intervals and formal paired/hierarchical estimates. Scenario labels A3, A5 and B1 must be defined or removed.
11. The paper alternates among “outside range,” “atypical,” “Competitive-Deviation” and “assertive-side alert.” A single nomenclature and an explicit two-sided decision rule would improve clarity; over-yielding results should not disappear without explanation.
12. Provide exact denominators for “one interaction moment in five,” “nearly nine of every ten scenarios,” “fewer than one judgeable moment in ten,” and every abstention statement, including the relevant filters.
13. The confirmatory/development/guard/calibration/test terminology is difficult to follow. A single partition diagram with sample counts and permitted uses is needed.
14. Data and code availability should identify a persistent repository, version, licence and release contents; “upon publication” is not sufficient for evaluation of the present analysis.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. What independent evidence shows that the IPV angle recovers a social preference rather than fit to a particular trajectory generator, and what is its test–retest or inter-model reliability?
2. What exact objectives, dynamics, constraints and counterpart assumptions generate each candidate trajectory? How sensitive are readings and flags to the seven-point grid, σ = 0.1, window length and alternative plausible forward models?
3. What is the exact formula for reliability and for the “sharpness” outcome in Fig. 2a? How were (u_0,q_0,c_0) selected without using test outcomes?
4. For every Fig. 5 endpoint, what timestamps enter the IPV estimate, alert persistence rule, baseline and outcome? Do any observed samples contribute to both alert and outcome?
5. How many unique scenes, interactions, runs, automated systems, alerts and participants contribute to each panel, and what dependence structure is respected by each confidence interval?
6. What adjusted model supports the statement that consequences were analysed “controlling for scenario and initial risk”? Please provide its estimand, covariates, coefficients, uncertainty and balance/diagnostic checks.
7. How do IPV alerts perform prospectively relative to TTC/PET, relative kinematics, trajectory-likelihood residuals and learned expectation/surprise baselines on the same held-out cases?
8. How were naturalistic scenes divided among train, guard, calibration and test? Can trajectories from the same physical scene, agent track or overlapping temporal interval appear in more than one split?
9. Given the near-zero or negative leave-one-source-out (R^2), on what statistical basis is the unchanged reference considered transported to the real-vehicle benchmark?
10. What equivalence margin and statistical test support “no alarm inflation” in the human arm? Could the 4.7% rate instead reflect over-wide intervals or differential abstention?
11. Were the 20 human drivers and 15 scenarios specified before seeing results? Please provide the registration, ethics approval, recruitment and randomisation details, and explain how participant and scenario dependence is modelled.
12. Why are over-yielding deviations not analysed symmetrically? Was the lack of a consequence signature a pre-specified null result, and was power adequate?
13. What is the measured end-to-end latency and throughput on hardware representative of deployment, including candidate generation and all gates?
14. Which results use the sealed confirmatory split, when was it opened, and which hypotheses and thresholds were frozen before it was examined?

## 5. Prioritised revision requests

1. **Establish construct validity.** Add an independent, held-out human-centred or experimentally manipulated validation linking IPV and its alerts to social preference/acceptability, and test sensitivity to alternative inverse models and simple behavioural constructs.
2. **Rebuild the consequence analysis prospectively.** Use non-overlapping exposure/outcome windows, first independent alerts, pre-trend/placebo checks, appropriate multi-level clustering and adjustment or matching on the full pre-alert situation; remove “followed by” claims unless this design supports them.
3. **Demonstrate incremental value.** Benchmark IPV alerts against collision-risk, kinematic, trajectory-likelihood and learned expectation/anomaly baselines and quantify what information the proposed monitor adds on held-out data.
4. **Validate calibration under clustering and shift.** Supply scene-disjoint partitions, group/source-conditional results, cluster-aware inference, source-held-out tests, and complete width–coverage–abstention trade-offs.
5. **Reanalyse and fully document the human arm.** Provide ethics and participant/protocol details, reason-specific abstention, hierarchical paired uncertainty, pre-specified equivalence testing and conclusions restricted to the sampled population and scenarios.
6. **Make the method reproducible and operationally testable.** Specify every model, feature, gate, threshold, split and statistical test; report runtime and robustness to map, identity, counterpart and multi-agent failures; release versioned code and analysis data for review.
7. **Correct the narrative and quantitative record.** Resolve the 40% versus 8–21% interval-width contradiction, remove placeholders and internal-file references, add all missing uncertainty and denominator information, and either supply normative evidence or retitle/reframe the work as conditional trajectory-atypicality monitoring.

## 6. Acceptance probability

- **As submitted:** 5%
- **Assuming a competent major revision:** 35%

The second probability remains limited because the most important remedies require new construct-validity evidence and a redesigned prospective validation, not only clarification or replotting.

## 7. Recommendation

**Reject.**

The proposed separation of estimation, abstention and context-conditioned reference intervals is conceptually useful, but the present evidence does not establish a socially meaningful or prospectively informative monitor. The central deficits—independent validation of the latent construct, avoidance of temporal/mechanical coupling, incremental comparison with ordinary kinematic monitors, and valid inference under clustered domain shift—are foundational.
