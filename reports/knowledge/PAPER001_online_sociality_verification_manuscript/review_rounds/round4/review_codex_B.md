# Reviewer B report

## 1. Summary assessment

This manuscript proposes a per-frame monitor that estimates an interaction preference value (IPV), abstains when the estimate or context is judged unsuitable, and compares accepted estimates with a situation-conditioned human reference interval. It reports narrower empirical intervals than a global reference, descriptive associations between assertive-side flags and subsequent interaction compression, and a matched-scenario human arm.

What is delivered is a data-driven conditional atypicality detector for a model-derived scalar. What is not yet delivered is a validated runtime-verification or assurance mechanism: the monitored property has no temporal alarm semantics, end-to-end real-time execution is not evaluated, the conformal calculation pools strongly dependent moments as if they were calibration units, uncertainty in the IPV estimate is not propagated to verdicts, and source-shift results show serious calibration failure. The human audit and consequence analyses also rely on repeated moment-level observations without adequate crossed-unit inference. These are not presentational concerns; they affect the meaning and reliability of the central claims. The paper could become important if recast and reanalysed as a selective statistical monitor with explicit operational limits, but it is not ready in its current form.

## 2. Major weaknesses

1. **[Location: Abstract; Introduction; Sections 2.1, 2.3 and 3; Algorithm 2] — “Online monitor” is used without an operational temporal semantics.** The evaluated output is an independent three-way decision at each accepted moment. The only conversion to a warning or planner signal is an optional persistence rule that is neither instantiated nor tested. Consequently, the paper gives no episode-level false-alarm probability, time to detection, warning duration, chattering rate, recovery semantics, or behaviour across abstention gaps. A nominal 10% pointwise outside rate can yield an alarm in almost every sufficiently long human episode, even when pointwise calibration is exact. This is decisive for runtime monitoring: a stream of correlated flags is not yet an operational monitor. The authors should define the temporal property and alarm automaton (including how gaps reset or suspend it), select the sequential error criterion, and report run-level false alarms, detection delay and warning duration on untouched data. A block/trajectory conformal construction, sequential conformal method, or explicitly non-guaranteed empirical persistence layer would all be defensible, but the present pointwise output must not be presented as deployment-ready runtime monitoring.

2. **[Location: Methods 4.4; Algorithm 1; Fig. 4b] — The stated conformal calibration does not have the claimed statistical footing.** The calibration radius is computed from 455,723 moment scores and evaluated on 461,937 test moments, although the manuscript explicitly acknowledges strong within-interaction dependence. Splitting whole scenes prevents train-test leakage; it does not make moments within a scene or interaction exchangeable calibration units. Thus the order statistic over hundreds of thousands of correlated frames is not a standard split-conformal guarantee, and near-nominal empirical test coverage does not “certify” the quantile model. Selection through readability/support gates also means the guarantee, if any, is only for the selected population under unchanged selection and exchangeability. Recalibration should use the independent sampling unit (scene, interaction or run), or a justified hierarchical/group/block conformal method. The paper should report the number of independent calibration and test units, cluster-weighted coverage, uncertainty across clusters and repeated splits, coverage by source and salient state group, and trajectory-wise coverage. Until then, “calibrated” should be qualified as empirical marginal coverage over accepted moments, with no finite-sample conformal guarantee established for the data structure used.

3. **[Location: Sections 2.1 and 2.3; Methods 4.1–4.4; Figs. 1, 2 and 4] — The measurement and verdict uncertainties are conflated.** The target being calibrated is itself the output of the same seven-candidate inverse-planning model used on the monitored vehicle. Concentration of candidate likelihoods—operationally, a maximum normalized weight just above 0.20—is called “reliability”, but concentration under a misspecified candidate family is not accuracy or identifiability. The monitor then compares a point estimate with a reference range of point estimates, without propagating estimator uncertainty or quantifying boundary-flip probability. A narrow human interval can therefore make verdicts more unstable even while improving empirical coverage of the model-derived score. The authors should (i) validate the IPV estimator against simulations or controlled interactions with known preference parameters and, preferably, independent human judgements; (ii) provide likelihood/posterior predictive diagnostics; (iii) test sensitivity to candidate grid, likelihood scale, window length, cost specification and reference geometry; and (iv) propagate the estimate set/distribution into a robust verdict, with an explicit indeterminate state when plausible IPV values cross a reference boundary. The present results support atypicality under one instrument, not reliable recovery of social preference.

4. **[Location: Abstract; Sections 2.2, 2.3, 2.5 and 3; Methods 4.4 and 4.6; Figs. 3c, 4 and 6] — The distribution-shift evidence contradicts the broad transfer language.** At nominal 90% coverage, leave-one-source-out coverage is 0.743 for Waymo and 0.750 for Lyft; Argoverse-2 obtains 0.900 only with 44.3% abstention. These are substantial failures, not a minor boundary. In the matched human arm, 4.7% of judgeable moments are outside a nominal 90% interval versus 9.72% in held-out natural driving. This shows severe overcoverage/loss of sensitivity in the new domain, even if alarms are not inflated. “No alarm inflation” is a one-sided and inadequate transfer criterion for a selective monitor. The authors should report, for every domain and arm, the complete funnel from candidate moments through each abstention reason to verdict; estimate calibration error and uncertainty at the independent run/driver/scenario level; and test equivalence to a predeclared tolerance rather than only absence of inflation. Either a shift-aware recalibration/adaptation scheme must be evaluated without outcome leakage, or the monitor’s scope must be restricted to the fitted domains and the new-country audit described as evidence of conservatism, not successful calibration transfer.

5. **[Location: Sections 2.5 and Methods 4.6; Fig. 6] — The central human-versus-automated comparison lacks an adequate sampling model and sufficient human-subject reporting.** The claim of 2.1-fold more flags is computed from repeated frame-level moments generated by 20 drivers, 19 automated systems and 15 scenarios. Fig. 6a and c explicitly omit uncertainty; “15/15 scenarios above parity” does not account for crossed driver/system and scenario effects, unequal readable exposure, or the dependence of consecutive moments. Moreover, the manuscript does not report recruitment, participant demographics and experience, run order/counterbalancing, acclimation, exclusions, completed/missing runs, compensation, ethics approval/consent, or the safety protocol. There are also 267 automated runs where a complete 19 × 15 matrix would contain 285. The remedy is a complete participant/protocol account and flow diagram, plus inference at the experimental unit: for example, a crossed hierarchical model or two-way cluster bootstrap over scenario and driver/system, with equal-weighted run-level endpoints, confidence intervals for all pairwise contrasts, and sensitivity to judgeable exposure. Without this, Fig. 6 cannot support a population comparison.

6. **[Location: Section 2.4; Methods 4.5; Fig. 5 and Fig. ED2] — The consequence analysis does not isolate a prospective consequence of a flag.** A flag is computed from the one-second trajectory window ending at time *t*, while TTC, speed and braking are functions of the same evolving interaction and begin at *t*. Repeated flags from one stretch create overlapping outcome windows, and within-range comparison moments can occur at systematically different interaction phases. Conditioning on readability, support, a defined closing margin and responsive-counterpart availability can further induce selection. Run-cluster bootstrapping does not remove this temporal confounding. The manuscript is appropriately cautious about causality, but wording such as “followed by” and “what a flag means” remains stronger than the design. The authors should use a landmarked analysis (for example, first eligible flag per interaction), non-overlapping future windows with a positive lag, matched risk sets on the complete pre-verdict state, balance diagnostics, and trajectory/run-level endpoints. Results should be replicated on a prospectively untouched set. At minimum, demonstrate that the association remains after excluding all samples contemporaneous with the estimator window and after controlling for interaction phase and pre-existing trend.

7. **[Location: Abstract; Sections 2.4 and 2.6; Methods 4.5; Fig. 5b,d] — “No rise in emergency rates” is not established.** The paper displays multiple nested TTC and braking thresholds, levels and windows with per-endpoint intervals and no multiplicity control. “Supported” is defined as an interval excluding zero, so the statement that rates are lower at every supported threshold is partly selection by significance. At TTC <3 s, the interval [−8.33, +3.15] percentage points permits a meaningful increase; non-significance cannot establish no increase. Nor does a lower observed tail rate validate safety, given the selection concerns above. The authors should predeclare one primary emergency endpoint, provide event counts and power, control the remaining battery or label it exploratory, and use a one-sided non-inferiority/equivalence test with an externally justified safety margin if the intended claim is “no increase”. Otherwise the correct conclusion is that no increase was detected under the analysed definitions, with some intervals too wide to exclude harm.

8. **[Location: Methods 4.1–4.4; Eq. 2–3; Algorithms 1–2] — The monitor contract is not reproducible or auditable from the manuscript.** The exact situation vector, online risk proxies, interaction-opportunity rule, quantile-regression model and hyperparameters, `q` and `c` gates, map/role uncertainty rule, source-health checks, OOD rule, tie-breaking and principal-counterpart logic are not specified. “Non-anticipation verified” is asserted without a timestamp/provenance audit. This matters because route conditioning, conflict-point assignment, counterpart identity and derived kinematics are plausible leakage channels, and because a formal monitor’s semantics depend on data age, coordinate frames and update order. Provide a feature-level contract table giving source, timestamp, units, availability deadline, missing-data behaviour and training status for every input; complete pseudocode for all gates and the fitted model; versioned frozen thresholds; and automated causal-leakage tests showing that perturbing future samples cannot change a verdict at time *t*.

9. **[Location: Introduction; Sections 2.6 and 3; “Planner interface”] — The runtime-assurance relationship is asserted rather than demonstrated.** Stating that the social layer is advisory and subordinate to collision safety does not prove non-interference. A soft cost, fallback request or hesitation warning can alter a trajectory, timing or controller mode; an abstention-triggered degraded mode can also create hazards. No scheduling bound, interface contract, safety invariant, arbitration rule, fault model or hazard analysis is given, and end-to-end execution on vehicle hardware is expressly unevaluated. The authors should either remove assurance/deployment implications and present the work as an offline replay study of an online-computable signal, or instantiate a runtime-assurance architecture with explicit assume–guarantee contracts: collision-safety shield authority, monitor deadline and stale-data policy, planner arbitration, fail-silent behaviour, and evidence that social actions cannot weaken the safety invariant. Latency and worst-case computational demand of seven candidate trajectory solves per agent per update must be measured on representative hardware.

10. **[Location: Title; Abstract; Introduction; Sections 2.4–3] — The construct established is conditional human atypicality, not social compliance.** The manuscript itself states that atypicality is neither inappropriateness nor harm, and no human preference, acceptability or norm-violation outcome is measured. The consequence signature is also unusual: ordinary margins compress while measured emergency events become less frequent, so it does not validate a straightforward adverse meaning. At NMI level, naming a model-derived departure from a sampled human distribution “social compliance” risks turning imitation into normativity despite repeated caveats. The title, abstract and contribution claims should consistently use “conditional social atypicality” unless an independent study shows that flags predict human dispreference, perceived inappropriateness or a predeclared interaction-harm construct. Such validation must also address culturally and geographically heterogeneous norms rather than treating one natural-driving reference as normative.

## 3. Minor issues

1. Reconcile all case counts in one flow table. Section 2.2 begins with 38,228 interactions, whereas Fig. 3b uses slightly smaller source-specific counts; Methods 4.4 then introduces 26,828 cases and several nested row universes.
2. Define the dependent variable and statistical model behind Fig. 2a completely. “Change in concentration relative to control pairings” is not sufficient to reconstruct the estimate or its clustered confidence interval.
3. Replace “reliability” with “candidate-weight concentration” unless accuracy calibration is added. The current terminology invites an unsupported epistemic interpretation.
4. Fig. 2b and Fig. 2c state that uncertainty is not shown. All quantities used to motivate design choices should have cluster-aware intervals and independent-unit counts.
5. Fig. 4c alternates between 486,660 total and 461,937 accepted moments. The graphic and caption should visually separate the estimands and denominators.
6. Report flag rates separately for assertive and over-yielding sides at every nominal level and in every arm; a combined two-sided rate can conceal asymmetric miscalibration.
7. Report the full negative/null results for over-yielding moments rather than only stating that they carry no consequence signature.
8. Clarify whether the same frame can contribute both agents as anchor rows and how such paired dependence is handled in fitting, calibration and resampling.
9. Explain how agent type is established in the natural corpora and substantiate the statement that automated data-collection vehicles cannot contaminate the human–human reference.
10. Specify whether confidence intervals are percentile, basic or bias-corrected bootstrap intervals and why 1,000 resamples are adequate for tail endpoints.
11. The term “supported threshold” is non-standard and potentially circular. Use “interval excludes zero” and distinguish it from safety support or assurance evidence.
12. The source-health input appears in Algorithm 2 but is never operationally defined. Enumerate health faults and their precedence among abstention reason codes.
13. Provide wall-clock and worst-case execution time, memory use, deadline-miss frequency and solver-failure handling. The naturalistic corpus already contains 1,826 solver failures.
14. Clarify the naming/order of Extended Data figures and correct the title “changes with with the situation” in the supplied reference-band figure.
15. Data and code availability only upon publication prevents independent assessment of the most consequential claims during review. At minimum, provide frozen analysis specifications, hashes and auditable source-data tables to editors/reviewers under appropriate access controls.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. What is the independent exchangeable unit for your conformal claim, and what theorem or simulation justifies treating dependent frame scores as the calibration order-statistic sample?
2. What are the episode-level probabilities of at least one false flag at the 80%, 90% and 95% levels, as a function of episode length and after the proposed persistence rule?
3. How often does IPV-estimation uncertainty cross a reference boundary, and how stable are verdicts to the seven-point grid, `sigma = 0.1 m`, window length and cost model?
4. Exactly which features enter `z_t` and every gate, and what evidence proves that none depends on future trajectory, realized passing order or a route inferred from the complete track?
5. Why should 74–75% leave-one-source-out coverage be regarded as compatible with a nominal 90% reference? What deployment action is taken when shift causes undercoverage rather than support abstention?
6. What are the human arm’s total candidate moments, readable moments, support abstentions by reason, judgeable moments and completed/excluded runs, for each nominal level and scenario?
7. What confidence interval results from treating driver/system and scenario—not frames—as crossed sampling units for the reported 2.1-fold difference?
8. Were all 20 human drivers exposed to all 15 scenarios? How were order, learning, fatigue and scenario familiarity handled, and what explains any missing human or automated runs?
9. What ethics approval, consent procedure and closed-course safety protocol governed the human experiment?
10. Does the consequence association survive using only the first eligible flag per interaction, a positive post-verdict lag, non-overlapping windows and matching on pre-verdict trend and interaction phase?
11. What non-inferiority margin would constitute an unacceptable increase in emergency events, and are the data powered to exclude it?
12. What exact safety mechanism prevents a planner action prompted by this monitor—including an abstention response—from weakening the collision-safety controller’s invariant?
13. Are human acceptability, discomfort or preference ratings available for any runs? If not, what empirical observation justifies “social compliance” beyond conditional human-likeness under the IPV instrument?

## 5. Prioritised revision requests

1. Rebuild calibration and uncertainty analysis at the scene/interaction/run level using a justified grouped or block conformal procedure; report cluster-aware and source-specific coverage plus abstention.
2. Define and evaluate a complete sequential monitor: temporal alarm rule, abstention semantics, episode false-alarm rate, detection delay, warning duration and untouched-test performance.
3. Reanalyse Figs. 5–6 at independent experimental units, with non-overlapping landmarked outcomes, crossed driver/system–scenario inference, complete uncertainty and multiplicity/non-inferiority treatment.
4. Provide complete human-subject methods, ethics information, run accounting and the full gating funnel for both human and automated arms.
5. Validate the IPV/readability instrument and propagate estimation uncertainty into verdicts; add sensitivity analyses for all consequential estimator choices.
6. Resolve the demonstrated distribution-shift failures through evaluated shift handling/recalibration, or sharply restrict the claimed validity domain and reinterpret the matched human arm as conservative miscalibration.
7. Supply a fully timed, non-anticipating monitor contract and either instantiate a compositional runtime-assurance interface or remove deployment/assurance implications.
8. Align the title and claims with conditional social atypicality unless independent human-normative validation is added.
