# Review of “Online monitoring of socially compliant autonomous driving”

## 1. Summary assessment

This manuscript proposes a frame-level monitor that estimates an interaction preference value (IPV) by inverse planning, abstains when that estimate or the human reference is insufficiently supported, and labels readable moments as inside or outside a situation-conditioned human range. It combines large naturalistic trajectory corpora with a matched-scenario real-vehicle benchmark and a human-driver arm. The paper is unusually explicit about abstention and about the distinction between atypicality, inappropriateness and harm. However, it currently establishes a calibrated detector of deviation in a model-defined trajectory statistic, not a monitor of social compliance in the substantive human-interaction sense used by the title. The IPV construct is not independently validated here; the consequence analyses are vulnerable to temporal and kinematic circularity; no comparison establishes value beyond simpler risk, trajectory-likelihood or interaction-anomaly baselines; and marginal moment-wise calibration does not support a sequential runtime alarm. Cross-source results also expose substantial distribution-shift failure. These are central, not presentational, limitations. The framework could become important, but the present evidence does not support the broad NMI-level claim or deployment interpretation.

## 2. Major weaknesses

1. **[Location: title, Abstract, Introduction, Sections 2.1–2.6 and 4.1] — The paper does not establish that IPV atypicality is social non-compliance.** IPV is produced by a specific inverse-planning model with a hand-specified utility combining own progress and an interaction term. Human trajectories are then passed through the same model to define the “human range”. This establishes self-consistency of one behavioural representation, but not construct validity for social compliance, courtesy, acceptability, predictability or human dispreference. The close agreement between online and offline estimates in Section 4.1 is agreement between two implementations of the same model, not validation against a latent preference. The authors appropriately acknowledge that atypicality is not inappropriateness, yet the title, opening motivation and several statements about “social compliance” still depend on precisely that unvalidated step. At NMI level, a new socially aware monitor must be tied to an independent measure of the social phenomenon, not only to the distribution of its own inferred score. **Remedy:** either reframe the paper throughout as online monitoring of context-conditioned *IPV atypicality*, including the title and contribution claims, or add independent construct validation: blinded human judgements of appropriateness/courtesy/predictability for sampled interaction clips; agreement with an independently specified social-preference measure; and tests showing that the IPV verdict predicts those judgements beyond current kinematics, interaction phase, scenario and safety margin.

2. **[Location: Sections 2.4, 2.6 and 4.5; Fig. 5 and Fig. ED2] — The “consequence signature” is not cleanly separated from the trajectory evidence that creates the flag.** Each verdict uses a one-second trajectory window ending at time `t`, while the outcome window begins at `t` and includes quantities strongly determined by the same evolving geometry. A low future TTC, counterpart deceleration or compressed margin can therefore be the continuation of an interaction already visible within the inference window rather than a consequence or prospectively informative correlate of the flag. Fig. ED2 makes this concern concrete: the counterpart’s abrupt speed fall begins before or around the selected flagged stretch. Conditioning within broad situation cells and permuting whole flag sequences does not rule out interaction phase, current closing rate, current TTC, conflict-point distance or regression-to-the-mean explanations. The reported case-level label permutation is non-significant (`p=0.1493`), whereas the preferred exposure-timing placebo uses only 200 draws and yields `p=0.0199`; this mixed evidence is not adequately resolved. **Remedy:** perform a pre-specified lead–lag/event-study analysis showing pre-flag trends and outcomes beginning after a non-overlapping washout interval; match or model flagged and within-range moments on current TTC, relative speed, distance to conflict, role, scenario and interaction phase; use within-run risk-set comparisons; report leave-one-run/scenario/system-out stability; and demonstrate prospective incremental prediction on a strictly held-out benchmark.

3. **[Location: Introduction; Sections 2.3–2.6; Methods 4.3–4.6] — There is no competitive baseline demonstrating that the proposed social monitor adds information beyond simpler observable interaction measures.** The reference model explicitly conditions on geometry, role, progress, relative kinematics and online risk proxies, and the reported consequences are TTC and speed/braking quantities. It is therefore essential to ask whether a direct monitor based on those same observable variables, a trajectory-likelihood/OOD score, a gap-acceptance model, or a standard interaction-risk metric identifies the same moments. The current contrast with collision thresholds is insufficient: showing that several hard emergency thresholds do not increase is not a comparison with continuous TTC, predicted post-encroachment margin, deceleration-to-safety-time, surprise/trajectory likelihood, or learned interaction anomaly. Without these controls, the proposed IPV layer may be an indirect and less transparent transformation of ordinary conflict geometry. **Remedy:** benchmark against strong continuous kinematic-risk, behaviour-likelihood and anomaly-detection baselines under identical abstention/coverage budgets. Quantify added explanatory or predictive value for independent human ratings and subsequent interaction outcomes after those baselines, with calibration, discrimination, coverage and computational cost reported on the same splits.

4. **[Location: Sections 2.3 and 4.4; Fig. 4] — The conformal and calibration claims are too strong for clustered, selected, sequential data.** The conformal radius is computed over 455,723 accepted *moments*, although moments within a scene and interaction are strongly dependent and scenes contribute unequal numbers of rows. Splitting whole scenes prevents direct leakage, but does not make all moment-level nonconformity scores exchangeable. Moreover, coverage is reported only after both readability and support selection; it is marginal over accepted moments, not conditional on situation, source, interaction or trajectory. The leave-one-source-out results show severe failures at the nominal 90% level (0.743 for Waymo and 0.750 for Lyft), alongside highly variable abstention. These results are important evidence against a generally calibrated human reference, not merely a boundary note. **Remedy:** calibrate and infer at the independent cluster level (or justify a hierarchical/block conformal procedure), report cluster-bootstrap uncertainty for coverage, and provide coverage–abstention curves by source, scenario, role, geometry, risk bin and interaction phase. The main claims must explicitly distinguish empirical in-distribution moment coverage from any formal or transferred guarantee.

5. **[Location: Sections 2.4 and 4.4–4.6; Figs. 5 and 6] — Per-moment flag rates and confidence intervals obscure the effective experimental units and repeated-testing behaviour.** The benchmark contains 267 automated-system runs (despite a nominal `19 × 15 = 285` design), only 175 non-scripted runs for counterpart outcomes, and 20 human drivers across 15 scenarios. Yet the visually dominant sample sizes are 14,099 and 15,102 judgeable moments, with many correlated flags per run. Run-bootstrap intervals help for some Fig. 5 endpoints, but Fig. 6a and 6c explicitly omit uncertainty, the 2.1-fold comparison is based on moments, and the drivers/systems/scenarios hierarchy is not modelled. “15/15 scenarios above parity” is not an inferential substitute for uncertainty across drivers, systems and unequal exposure durations. **Remedy:** make the run, driver and automated system the primary units; explain all missing cells; report per-run and per-episode alarm probabilities, hierarchical estimates with driver/system and scenario effects, confidence intervals for every cross-population comparison, and sensitivity to equal weighting of runs rather than moments. Separate descriptive moment counts from independent sample sizes everywhere.

6. **[Location: Abstract; Sections 2.5, 3 and 4.6; Fig. 6] — The human-reference arm does not establish transfer “across country, apparatus and task”.** A lower outside-range rate in the matched human arm (4.7%) than in held-out natural driving (9.72%) does not validate transfer. It may instead reflect a wider-than-needed reference, selective support/readability, conservative closed-course behaviour, repeated exposure to 15 staged scenarios, or a different mixture of situations. Calibration requires the expected rate under the target distribution with uncertainty and an account of abstention, not merely absence of alarm inflation. This is especially problematic given the poor leave-one-natural-source coverage reported in Section 4.4. The matched human arm is valuable, but it supports a limited apparatus/scenario comparison, not broad geographic transfer. **Remedy:** report target-arm coverage and abstention by driver and scenario at all nominal levels, with cluster-aware intervals; compare the distributions of accepted states and range widths; perform calibration or recalibration analyses under the target shift; and revise the transfer claim to the precise tested population and scenario set unless genuinely independent geographic replication is added.

7. **[Location: Sections 2.4 and 2.6; Fig. 5b,d] — “No rise in emergency rates” is not established by the reported interval logic.** Negative point estimates at nested thresholds show that emergency events are not more frequent in this sample, but a confidence interval containing zero at the `<3 s` threshold does not demonstrate absence of an increase. Conversely, per-endpoint intervals excluding zero across several nested thresholds are not a simultaneous family-wise statement. Calling thresholds “supported” after inspecting whether each interval excludes zero risks converting uncertainty into a selection rule, even if the battery was frozen. The result also sits awkwardly with the proposed interpretation: flagged “assertive” moments appear to have *fewer* emergency events and essentially unchanged lower-quartile margin, making normative concern less, not more, evident. **Remedy:** state the estimand and multiplicity policy; use simultaneous intervals or a pre-specified primary endpoint; test non-inferiority/equivalence against an explicitly justified safety margin if the intended claim is no increase; and avoid equating a non-significant difference with evidence of absence. Explain how compression of ordinary margins, coupled with rarer emergency tails, should be interpreted socially without presupposing that larger margins are always preferable.

8. **[Location: Sections 2.1, 4.1–4.4 and Algorithms 1–2] — The estimator and gates are insufficiently specified and insufficiently stress-tested for the central technical contribution.** Essential definitions remain qualitative: the own-progress and interaction costs, trajectory generator and solver, interaction-opportunity horizon, `q0` and `c0`, map/role uncertainty rules, quantile-regression model and hyperparameters, support feature scaling, target-proximal action exclusions, and model-selection procedure. Saying these will be released in code does not make the manuscript technically self-contained. The seven-point preference grid, `σ=0.1 m` likelihood scale, one-second window, maximum-weight threshold of 0.20 and `k=25` support rule can materially determine both readability and verdicts, yet no sensitivity or identification study is presented. Solver failures also occur in the natural corpus. **Remedy:** provide full mathematical definitions and all frozen thresholds in the paper or supplement; show recovery and abstention behaviour in controlled interactions with known generating preferences; report sensitivity to grid density, likelihood scale, window length, map/reference error and trajectory-model misspecification; and propagate perception, tracking and counterpart-identity uncertainty into both IPV estimates and verdicts.

9. **[Location: Abstract; Sections 2.4, 3 and 4.1–4.6] — “Online” is currently an information-timing property, not a demonstrated runtime monitor.** The manuscript explicitly states that end-to-end real-time operation on vehicle hardware is not evaluated. Yet the contribution is repeatedly framed as a pluggable runtime layer, while only 20.8% of benchmark candidate moments receive a verdict, 79.2% abstain, and the proposed persistence/warning layer is not evaluated. A monitor that speaks at least once in 86.5% of runs may still be silent at the decision-relevant time. No latency, throughput, resource, sensor-noise, map-failure, warning-duration, false-alert-per-run or detection-delay results are given. These are essential for a runtime-monitor contribution rather than an offline causal replay. **Remedy:** report end-to-end causal replay timing and preferably on-vehicle timing; quantify verdict availability aligned to conflict evolution; evaluate sequential persistence rules, episode-level false alarms, detection delay and warning duration; and stress-test noisy perception, identity switches, uncertain route/priority and missing maps. Otherwise consistently describe the work as an offline evaluation of an online-computable statistic.

## 3. Minor issues

1. The manuscript alternates among “IPV”, “preference”, “reading”, “social behaviour”, “competitive deviation” and “assertive-side atypicality”. A terminology table should distinguish the estimator output, the reference-set membership decision and the social interpretation.

2. Fig. 2a does not define the plotted concentration statistic sufficiently on the axis or in the caption; the meaning and units of changes such as (-0.132) are not recoverable from the figure alone.

3. Fig. 2b and Fig. 6a,c omit uncertainty. These are central comparisons, not illustrative quantities, and uncertainty should be displayed.

4. Fig. 3a uses realised post-encroachment time to describe context effects. Although the caption correctly says it is offline-only, the main text should more sharply separate this descriptive result from evidence about the online feature set.

5. The statement that a scalar sociality score is “ill-posed” is stronger than the evidence. State dependence shows loss under aggregation, not that every scalar summary lacks a valid, explicitly defined use.

6. Fig. 4a normalises widths by the seven-candidate span rather than the declared IPV parameter domain. This choice is explained, but the visual claim that the global interval covers the “entire admissible range” can be misread as the full `[-π/2, π/2]` domain.

7. The manuscript should report the distribution of consecutive flag-stretch lengths and the number of distinct flagged episodes, not only flagged frames and runs.

8. The 36 of 267 automated-system runs with no verdict should be tabulated by scenario/system and reason. “Readable at least once” is not enough to establish that human-support failure is benign.

9. Fig. ED2 is transparently selected, but its filter leaves only two eligible runs and then shows the more extreme one. It should remain strictly illustrative and not be used rhetorically to reinforce the population result.

10. Data and code are promised only upon publication. For a method whose results depend on many frozen gates and crosswalks, review-stage access to executable code, split manifests and derived verdict tables would materially improve verifiability.

11. The Abstract phrase “with no rise in emergency rates at any supported threshold” should be replaced by a direct descriptive statement with uncertainty and the unsupported `<3 s` result acknowledged.

12. The manuscript should state whether the 20 human drivers encountered scenario order randomisation, practice effects or repeated exposure, and whether analysts were blinded to driver/system identity during endpoint extraction.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. What independent evidence shows that IPV, rather than merely the chosen inverse-planning utility, measures a socially meaningful preference? Are human ratings, stated preferences or controlled-game ground truths available for any analysed interactions?

2. How much incremental information does the verdict provide beyond current TTC, relative speed, conflict-point distance, PET proxies, interaction phase and a generic trajectory-likelihood/OOD score?

3. Precisely which samples overlap between the one-second IPV inference window and each post-verdict endpoint? Do results persist when outcomes begin 0.5, 1.0 or 2.0 s after the verdict and when pre-flag trends are controlled?

4. Why should all accepted calibration moments be treated as exchangeable conformal scores despite strong within-scene and within-interaction dependence? What coverage results obtain under scene- or interaction-level calibration?

5. What are the effective numbers of independent flagged and within-range runs, systems, drivers and scenarios for every Fig. 5 and Fig. 6 comparison? How sensitive are results to equal run weighting?

6. Why are only 267 of the nominal 285 automated-system-by-scenario runs present, and are missing cells related to system performance, scenario difficulty or monitor availability?

7. What fraction of active conflict time, rather than all candidate moments, is judgeable for each scenario and population? At what time relative to closest approach does the first useful verdict occur?

8. How do per-driver and per-scenario outside rates and abstention rates in the human arm compare with the nominal levels? Is 4.7% statistically compatible with the intended 10% outside rate after clustering?

9. What model class performs the conditional quantile regression, how were hyperparameters selected, and which exact features and thresholds enter every gate? Were any choices modified after seeing test or benchmark results?

10. Do the headline findings survive alternative IPV candidate grids, likelihood scales, temporal windows, trajectory generators and plausible map/role/perception errors?

11. For the counterpart-speed results, how often had deceleration begun before the first flag? Can a pre-trend-balanced analysis distinguish response to the ego’s behaviour from the monitor detecting an interaction already tightening?

12. What is the intended operational action when an assertive-side flag coincides with lower observed emergency rates? What evidence justifies intervention rather than simple audit logging?

13. How were the four nested emergency-margin thresholds and three braking thresholds chosen, and what is the pre-specified primary endpoint and multiplicity control?

14. Can the authors provide runtime latency, hardware specification and solver-tail statistics, including the effect of the 1,826 natural-corpus solver failures on selection and calibration?

## 5. Prioritised revision requests

1. **Establish or narrow the construct.** Add independent human-facing validation of IPV verdicts and their incremental validity, or retitle and reframe the work as monitoring context-conditioned IPV atypicality rather than social compliance.

2. **Break the temporal and kinematic circularity.** Reanalyse consequences with non-overlapping prospective windows, lead–lag diagnostics, strong current-state matching/adjustment and held-out run/scenario/system validation.

3. **Add competitive baselines.** Compare against continuous risk metrics, trajectory-likelihood/OOD and interaction-anomaly baselines at matched coverage and abstention, and quantify incremental value.

4. **Repair the inferential unit and calibration analysis.** Use cluster-aware or hierarchical calibration/inference; report uncertainty at run, driver, system and scenario levels; and expose dependence on moment weighting.

5. **Evaluate the actual sequential monitor.** Report per-episode false alarms, consecutive-alert behaviour, detection delay, warning duration, decision-time availability and end-to-end runtime under realistic input uncertainty.

6. **Bound transfer claims to the evidence.** Present the severe leave-one-source-out failures as a principal result, fully analyse coverage and abstention in the matched human arm, and remove broad geographic/apparatus transfer language unless externally replicated.

7. **Make the method reproducible from the manuscript.** Specify the utility, trajectory generator, quantile model, all features/gates/thresholds and frozen analysis choices, with robustness to the main modelling parameters.

8. **Correct the emergency-tail claims.** Use an explicit primary endpoint and simultaneous or equivalence/non-inferiority inference; revise all “no rise” language to match what the intervals actually establish.

## 6. Acceptance probability

- **As submitted:** 8%.
- **Assuming a competent major revision:** 38%. This would require new validation and substantial reanalysis, not only clarification or rewriting.

## 7. Recommendation

**Reject.** The monitoring architecture and abstention discipline are promising, but the present manuscript does not yet validate the social construct named in the title, demonstrate advantage over simpler interaction-state baselines, or establish a deployment-relevant sequential monitor. Those gaps require new evidence substantial enough that I would favour resubmission after reconstruction rather than an ordinary revision cycle.
