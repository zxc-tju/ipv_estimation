# Reviewer D — Nature Machine Intelligence

## 1. Summary assessment

The manuscript reframes social compliance of autonomous vehicles as online monitoring of "conditional social atypicality": an inverse-planning preference estimate (IPV) is read per frame behind an explicit readability/abstention gate, judged against a situation-conditioned human reference range calibrated by split-conformal prediction, and then taken to a matched-scenario real-vehicle benchmark where flagged-assertive moments show a compression of interaction quality without added emergencies, and where the reference is audited by human drivers in the same scenarios (humans flagged 4.7%, automated systems 9.8%, roughly 2.1x). The framing is genuinely fresh — clinical-style reference ranges plus principled abstention applied to machine social behaviour — the epistemic discipline is exemplary, and the audit-by-defining-population design is elegant. However, the paper's central construct never faces a competitor: no baseline monitor is evaluated, so the added value of the preference layer over conditional kinematic atypicality is unestablished; the majority flag class (over-yielding, 63% of flags) has no demonstrated meaning; the benchmark, the 19 driving systems, and the human-driver protocol are barely described; ethics statements are absent; and the normative links of the paper's own validation chain remain promissory. A serious, salvageable submission that is not yet at NMI evidential standard.

## 2. Major weaknesses

**M1. The monitor is never compared against any baseline, so the distinctive value of the IPV layer is unestablished.** [Sections 2.4, 2.6; Fig. 5] The paper's only comparator is emergency-threshold checking (Section 2.6), which is a weak foil: emergency thresholds are not designed to detect graded compression. The decisive control is absent: does a conditional reference range over directly observable kinematics (closing rate, gap dynamics, acceleration given the same situation cells), or an existing learned-behaviour deviation monitor (the authors cite refs. [45]-[47] but never test against them), reproduce the Fig. 5 consequence signature? This matters doubly because flag and outcome are mechanically coupled: the trajectory features that make a window match low-theta candidates (Eq. 1) are autocorrelated with the kinematics that produce a smaller post-verdict TTC and counterpart braking. Within-cell comparison and the run-level placebo address timing and coarse context, not this fine-grained coupling. At NMI, "we built a monitor" requires "the monitor sees something simpler instruments do not." Remedy: implement at least one kinematic-atypicality baseline and one prior anomaly/deviation method under the identical frozen protocol, and report whether the compression signature and the human/AV audit separation survive as unique to the preference layer.

**M2. The majority of flags (over-yielding: 869 of 1,388, 63%) have no validated meaning.** [Section 2.4, p.12; Methods 4.4] The entire consequence analysis concerns the assertive side (519 flags); the over-yielding side receives one sentence ("carries no such signature on either side"). Over-yielding is precisely the failure mode most associated with deployed AVs (hesitant, obstructive behaviour), and the planner interface (p.22) maps it to a hesitation/efficiency warning — yet no efficiency, delay, follower-pressure, or counterpart-confusion outcome is ever examined. As it stands, the monitor's most frequent verdict is uninterpreted, which undermines the claim of an auditable runtime monitor. Remedy: either provide an outcome analysis for over-yielding flags (efficiency and interaction-burden endpoints are computable from the same logs) or explicitly demote that verdict class to exploratory status in abstract, Results, and the planner interface.

**M3. The real-vehicle benchmark, the 19 driving systems, and the human-driver protocol are critically under-described, and the headline "2.1x" inherits every one of these unknowns.** [Sections 2.4-2.5; Methods 4.5-4.6; ref. 53] The reader is never told what the 15 scenario templates are (cut-ins? unprotected turns?), what the course, vehicle, instrumentation, and counterpart scripting were, or — most importantly — what the 19 "independent driving systems" are. If they are competition entries of research-prototype maturity, "automated systems are flagged about twice as often as matched human drivers" characterises immature stacks, not automated driving, and the abstract's most quotable number changes meaning entirely. Symmetrically, nothing is reported about driver briefing, incentives, scenario order, or naivety; supervised drivers on an instrumented test course plausibly drive more conservatively than the naturalistic reference population, which could produce the low human flag rate (4.7%) by protocol rather than by typicality. The citation for the benchmark is a bare URL. Remedy: a full benchmark description (scenario taxonomy, apparatus, scripting, system provenance and maturity class, driver protocol), an archival reference, and explicit hedging of the 2.1x claim to the population actually tested.

**M4. Run and denominator accounting has unexplained gaps.** [Methods 4.5, p.21; Section 2.4, p.11] 19 systems x 15 scenarios = 285, but 267 runs are reported, with no explanation of the 18 missing. Excluding the 27 scripted-counterpart runs leaves 240, yet consequence analyses use "the 175 non-scripted scenario runs with recorded outcomes" — 65 runs (27%) lack recorded outcomes with no stated reason and no analysis of whether missingness correlates with flags. Similarly, 47 of 519 assertive flags lack a defined post-verdict margin and are excluded; since undefined TTC arises when paths are "already diverging" (i.e., the loosest interactions), this exclusion is not obviously outcome-neutral, and the human arm's candidate-moment denominator (the analogue of 67,861) is never given. Judgeability itself differs across arms (discriminative fraction 55.3% benchmark vs 70.3% natural), so flag rates conditioned on judgeable moments inherit a selection layer that identical gate treatment does not equalise. Remedy: a complete accounting table (runs, moments, exclusions, reasons, per arm), a missingness analysis, and a sensitivity analysis with alternative denominators (flags per run, per active-interaction second).

**M5. Mandatory ethics, consent, and safety reporting is missing.** [Methods 4.6; p.33] Twenty licensed drivers drove deliberately staged conflict scenarios against scripted counterpart injections in a real vehicle. There is no ethics-approval statement, no informed-consent statement, no safety protocol, and no institutional oversight named; pseudonymisation is mentioned but is not a substitute. Author Contributions and Acknowledgements are empty. At any Nature-family journal this is a hard, non-negotiable requirement, independent of scientific merit. Remedy: full ethics/consent/safety statements and completed declarations; if approval was obtained under the benchmark organisers' framework, say so with the approval identifier.

**M6. The transfer story is internally tense, and the on-course audit is too aggregate to carry it.** [Methods 4.4, p.20; Fig. 3c; Section 2.5; Fig. 6] Leave-one-source-out coverage at the 90% level is 0.743 (Waymo) and 0.750 (Lyft) — substantial under-coverage on two of four sources — and the cross-source predictive structure of the situation surface is nil (out-of-source R2 at or below zero, Fig. 3c). The reference is nonetheless carried across country, apparatus, and task, and declared to "survive the move" on the strength of an aggregate flag rate (4.7% vs the 9.72% native rate) plus a consequence signature. But the acceptance criterion is asymmetric: under-flagging by a factor of two is narrated as success ("does not inflate its alarms"), whereas an insensitive instrument would pass the same test. Aggregate rates can also mask per-situation mis-centring that cancels in the mean. Remedy: per-situation-cell outside-rate diagnostics in the human arm (15,102 judgeable moments make this feasible); a pre-stated two-sided acceptance band for the on-course human outside rate; and an explicit reconciliation of the LOSO under-coverage with the deployment claim.

**M7. The "adds nothing" ablation claims and the "no hidden intention" conclusion outrun the reported statistics.** [Section 2.3, p.10; Methods 4.3, p.19; Discussion, p.16] The counterpart-IPV ablation is reported as a null (paired 90% interval-score difference -0.0002, p = 0.86) without a confidence interval or minimum detectable effect, and "does not measurably sharpen" is elsewhere upgraded to "adds nothing beyond the current situation." Two further problems: (i) the counterpart channel uses the same noisy online estimator, so attenuation could mask real value — an oracle (offline) counterpart-IPV ablation is the informative version; (ii) z_t already contains relative kinematics and risk proxies, i.e., the counterpart's observable behaviour — which is exactly the channel through which agents read intention — so the Discussion's philosophically flavoured conclusion that a social monitor "does not need to read the other agent's hidden intention" is closer to "explicit latent-preference estimates add nothing once the partner's observable behaviour is conditioned on." Remedy: equivalence-grade reporting (CI, MDE) for both ablations, an oracle-counterpart variant, and a rewritten Discussion paragraph that states the bounded finding.

**M8. Per-moment verdicts rest on a highly volatile estimate, and the persistence layer that would make flags operational is asserted but never evaluated.** [Fig. 2b; Methods 4.2; Algorithm 2, line 15; Fig. ED2] The reading moves 0.30 rad between consecutive frames where most readable — roughly three-quarters of a candidate step every 0.1 s, against a reference range of mean width 1.87 rad. The paper embraces volatility ("readable does not mean settled") and calibration is marginal, so coverage holds; but the operational meaning of an isolated single-frame flag is then unclear, and ED2's selection hints that sustained flag runs are rare (display criteria including at least five contiguous flagged frames are met by 2 of 120 flagged runs, albeit conjoined with other criteria). The sequential-warning layer is "tuned on an independent guard set" and never characterised or evaluated anywhere. Remedy: report the flag run-length distribution, separate isolated-frame from sustained flags in the consequence analysis, and evaluate the persistence/warning layer that Algorithm 2 ships.

**M9. Uncertainty is omitted from headline display panels, and the validation chain's normative links remain promissory.** [Figs. 2b, 6a, 6c; abstract; Section 3] Three panels carry the annotation "uncertainty not shown," two of them in the audit figure that carries the paper's most consequential comparisons (three-population flag rates; 15/15 per-scenario separation); honest annotation is not a remedy at this level, and for the human-arm signature it is not stated which quantities' intervals exclude parity. More broadly, the paper's own chain — reference range, atypicality, human-dispreferred, interaction-harmful — is delivered only to its first two links: harm is explicitly "bounded rather than confirmed," and no human-dispreference evidence (ratings, perception, complaint data) appears at all, so the "social" semantics of the flag rest on interpretation plus the Fig. 5 correlates. The restraint is admirable; the residual deliverable is an atypicality detector for a derived parameter. Remedy: case-clustered intervals on all comparative display panels; and either one concrete dispreference result (even a small human-rating study on flagged vs matched within-range moments) or a further tempered abstract.

## 3. Minor issues

1. u_a(t) is introduced as a "reliability measure" given by weight concentration (p.5) but is a dispersion statistic in Methods (p.17), which is what makes the gate u_a < u_0 coherent; define it once, with direction.
2. "Admissible range" is ambiguous: theta is defined on [-pi/2, pi/2] (p.5), but Fig. 4a's percentages (mean width 1.87 rad = 79%) imply the candidate span of 2.356 rad. State which, in the figure and Methods.
3. Fig. 4a annotation consistency: 79/100 is a 21% narrowing, but text and caption say 20% at the 90% level (while 62/78 = 21% is correctly reported at 80%); harmonise the rounding.
4. "9.845%" is false precision (1,388/14,099) and inconsistent with "4.7%"; report one decimal throughout.
5. "It abstains on only a small fraction of supported cases" (p.9) refers to support abstention (5.08%) but is easily read as overall abstention; on the benchmark, verdicts issue on about one in five candidate moments. Clarify scope, and give the benchmark's abstention reason breakdown as is done for the natural corpus.
6. The case/row bookkeeping (38,228 cases; 26,828 reference cases; 3,695,981 frames; 4,497,368 anchor rows; 2,442,625 reference rows) is reconstructible but only by reverse-engineering; add a data-flow table.
7. Fig. 3a effect sizes (+0.058 to -0.034 rad) are 2-3% of the 90% reference-range width; the sign reversal is real but the prose ("a scalar sociality score is ill-posed") should acknowledge magnitude, since the operative evidence for conditioning is Fig. 4's width reduction.
8. Fig. 2a's axis convention (negative = more sharply identified) forces caption re-reading; consider flipping the sign or axis.
9. Online-offline estimator agreement (r = 0.993) is "bounded to the lane-referenced slice (about three quarters of cases)"; the agreement on the remaining quarter is not quantified, yet those cases receive verdicts.
10. "Pre-registration" (Methods 4.6) and "frozen before this study" (4.4) are claimed without a citable, time-stamped artifact; link a registration or rephrase as "pre-specified."
11. Duplicated "References" heading (p.26); the "Extended Data" heading appears after Fig. ED1; empty section stubs on p.33 — draft-state artifacts.
12. Fig. 1a: the conflict-zone marker is nearly invisible and the time labels crowd the trajectory.
13. Ref. [53] is a URL-only citation for the load-bearing benchmark.
14. The abstract's closing "which the monitor makes testable" is a promissory claim; consider "renders testable in stated next steps" framing or drop.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. What are the 19 driving systems — provenance, maturity, algorithmic diversity? Would you defend "automated systems are flagged about twice as often" as a statement about automated driving generally, or only about this population?
2. What were the human drivers' instructions, incentives, prior knowledge of scenarios, and exposure order? Was a safety driver or supervisor present, and could the protocol itself induce conservative driving?
3. Why do 65 of 240 non-scripted runs lack recorded outcomes, and why were 18 of 285 system-scenario runs never produced? Does outcome missingness correlate with flag status?
4. Does a conditional reference range over directly observable kinematics (same cells, same conformal machinery) reproduce the Fig. 5 compression signature and the human/AV flag-rate separation? If yes, what does the IPV layer add; if no, show it.
5. What is the operational meaning of an over-yielding flag, given that 63% of flags are over-yielding and no consequence or efficiency signature is reported for them?
6. What is the confidence interval and minimum detectable effect for the counterpart-IPV and self-history ablations, and does an oracle (offline-estimated) counterpart preference also add nothing?
7. Is the human arm's 4.7% outside rate uniform across situation cells, or concentrated in cells where the carried reference is mis-centred? Why is a two-fold under-flagging acceptable evidence of instrument validity when a two-fold over-flagging would presumably not be?
8. What is the distribution of flag run lengths, what fraction of flags are isolated single frames, and how does the tuned persistence layer transform them into warnings in practice?
9. What is the human arm's candidate-moment denominator, and which human-arm consequence quantities in Fig. 6b have intervals excluding parity?
10. Given LOSO coverage of 0.743-0.750 on two of four sources, what argument, beyond the aggregate on-course audit, supports carrying the pooled reference across country, apparatus, and task?

## 5. Prioritised revision requests

1. Add complete ethics, consent, and safety statements and completed author declarations (M5). Blocking and cheap.
2. Implement baseline comparisons — at minimum a conditional kinematic-atypicality monitor and one prior deviation/anomaly method under the frozen protocol — and report whether the consequence signature and audit separations are specific to the IPV layer (M1).
3. Fully document the benchmark, systems, and human-driver protocol; add an archival benchmark reference; close the run/denominator accounting including missing-outcome analysis and alternative-denominator sensitivity (M3, M4).
4. Analyse the over-yielding flag class against efficiency/burden outcomes, or demote it to exploratory throughout (M2).
5. Provide per-cell on-course coverage diagnostics, a pre-stated two-sided acceptance criterion for the human-arm outside rate, and a reconciliation with LOSO under-coverage (M6).
6. Upgrade the ablation nulls to equivalence-grade reporting with an oracle-counterpart variant, and rewrite the "hidden intention" Discussion claim to its bounded form (M7).
7. Report flag run-length structure and evaluate the persistence/warning layer (M8).
8. Add case-clustered uncertainty to Figs. 2b, 6a, 6c; state which human-arm signature intervals exclude parity; fix precision, label, and accounting inconsistencies (M9, minors 2-6).
9. Either supply one piece of human-dispreference evidence or further temper the abstract's framing of the validation chain (M9).

## 6. Acceptance probability

(a) As submitted: **~7%.** The missing ethics reporting alone is disqualifying as-is, and the absence of any baseline leaves the central claim of distinctive value untested.

(b) Assuming a competent major revision that delivers requests 1-6 (in particular, a baseline comparison in which the IPV layer retains unique signal): **~45%.** The framing, data assets, and audit design are strong enough to clear the bar if the added value survives the controls; if the kinematic baseline reproduces the signature, the paper becomes a methods contribution better suited to a specialist venue.

## 7. Recommendation

**Major revision.**
