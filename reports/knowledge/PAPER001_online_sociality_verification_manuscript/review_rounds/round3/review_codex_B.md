# Reviewer B report

## 1. Summary assessment

This manuscript proposes a three-valued online monitor that estimates an interaction preference value (IPV), abstains when the estimate or human support is inadequate, and otherwise tests the estimate against a situation-conditioned human reference interval. It delivers a large naturalistic reference analysis, a split-conformal interval construction, a matched-scenario vehicle study, and a human comparison arm. The defensible result is narrower than the title: the system detects departures from the distribution of outputs produced by one inverse-planning model on selected, supported pairwise interactions. The manuscript does not yet establish that IPV is a valid measurement of social preference, that moment-level conformal calibration yields meaningful runtime false-alarm control, or that the observed post-flag associations are specific to the proposed monitor. Moreover, “online” currently means only non-anticipatory inputs; execution deadlines, sequential alarm behaviour and integration within a runtime-assurance architecture are untested. These are central, not cosmetic, limitations. The abstention discipline and explicit separation of atypicality from harm are appropriate, but the evidence does not support the broader framing of monitoring “socially compliant autonomous driving.” I would require a major revision with substantially stronger measurement validation, sequence-aware calibration, statistical analysis and claim narrowing.

## 2. Major weaknesses

1. **[Location: title, Abstract, Introduction, Sections 2.1, 2.3 and 3] — The monitored construct is not validated as social preference or social compliance.** IPV is inferred through a seven-candidate inverse-planning model whose utility, trajectory generator and likelihood jointly define the reported quantity. The human reference is then built from outputs of the same estimator. The controls in Fig. 2a show that candidate weights are more concentrated during aligned interactions; they do not show that the selected candidate is correct, that the numerical value recovers a preference, or that the ordering “more negative = more assertive” agrees with human judgements. Thus, the current construction can detect model-relative trajectory atypicality, but a flag may also reflect planner-model mismatch, map-reference error or an unmodelled behavioural mode. This matters because the paper repeatedly names the object “social preference” and the overall task “social compliance,” even while correctly conceding that atypicality is not inappropriateness. Remedy: provide independent construct validation, for example controlled interactions or simulations with known preference parameters plus blinded human ratings of assertiveness/acceptability and convergent behavioural measures. At minimum, show recovery, monotonicity, inter-rater agreement and sensitivity to plausible model misspecification. If such evidence is unavailable, retitle and consistently reframe the contribution as monitoring conditional human-trajectory atypicality under a specified inverse-planning representation.

2. **[Location: Sections 2.3 and 4.4; Fig. 4; Algorithm 1] — The stated conformal construction does not provide the runtime guarantee readers are likely to infer.** The conformal radius is computed from 455,723 accepted *moments*, although moments are strongly dependent within interactions and scenes. Splitting whole scenes prevents train/test leakage but does not make all moment-level nonconformity scores exchangeable; longer interactions also receive greater weight. The manuscript acknowledges that coverage is only empirical and pointwise marginal over accepted moments, but continues to call the monitor calibrated without making clear that standard finite-sample split-conformal coverage does not follow for this sampling unit. More importantly, 90% pointwise coverage says nothing about the probability of one or more false flags in a run, the duration of false alarms, or the false-alarm rate after a persistence rule. For a stream monitor, these are the operational quantities. Remedy: state the exact probabilistic claim and its assumptions; calibrate/resample at the independent scene or interaction level, or use a hierarchical/cluster-valid construction; and report trace-level operating characteristics. A pre-specified sequential layer should be evaluated for per-run false-alarm probability, warning duration and detection delay. Simultaneous or time-uniform coverage is required if the interval is to carry an assurance interpretation. If only empirical pointwise calibration is retained, the terminology and claims must be narrowed accordingly.

3. **[Location: Sections 2.3, 2.5, 3 and 4.4; Figs. 4 and 6] — The distribution-shift evidence contradicts a general transfer claim, and abstention can mask miscalibration.** At nominal 90% coverage, leave-one-source-out coverage is 0.743 for Waymo and 0.750 for Lyft; nuPlan overcovers at 0.990, while Argoverse-2 reaches 0.900 only with 44.3% abstention. These are large failures, not a secondary boundary. The matched human arm's 4.7% flag rate, compared with 9.72% in held-out natural driving, does not establish that the reference “transfers across country, apparatus and task.” A lower flag rate can reflect over-wide intervals, a changed accepted population, or selective abstention. Judgeable-moment denominators alone do not expose how the gate changes the state distribution. Remedy: report active, readable, supported and judged fractions separately for natural humans, course humans and automated systems, with reason-specific abstention and covariate coverage. Provide calibration curves and uncertainty at all nominal levels in the target domain, including results before and after selection. The transfer claim should be restricted to the 15 staged scenarios unless an external-source calibration or distribution-shift method is prospectively validated. Coverage and abstention must be evaluated jointly, with a risk-coverage or alarm-coverage curve rather than a single accepted-set number.

4. **[Location: Sections 2.4, 4.5 and Extended Data Fig. ED2; Fig. 5] — The consequence analysis does not isolate information added by the social-atypicality verdict.** The situation vector contains relative kinematics and online risk proxies that are themselves related to future time-to-collision and braking. The ego IPV likelihood also conditions on the counterpart trajectory in the one-second window ending at the verdict. Consequently, a flag may be generated after a tightening interaction or counterpart response has already begun; ED2 appears consistent with this concern. Beginning the outcome window at the verdict avoids literal future leakage but does not establish temporal precedence of the relevant behavioural change. Nor does comparison “within frozen situation cells,” as currently underspecified, establish that the verdict adds information beyond raw IPV, current kinematics, time-to-conflict, or a conventional anomaly score. Remedy: show pre-flag trajectories and event-study estimates; impose a lag or washout so outcome samples do not overlap the causal history used by the estimator; match or model on the complete situation vector and pre-trends; and quantify incremental predictive value over kinematic risk, raw IPV, global-range and learned-behaviour baselines at matched coverage. Whole-sequence timing placebos should be reported for every primary endpoint, not only as a single omnibus statement. The language “is followed by” should remain explicitly associational unless these tests succeed.

5. **[Location: Sections 2.4, 2.5, 4.5 and 4.6; Figs. 5 and 6] — The inferential unit does not match the crossed experimental design.** Thousands of overlapping moment windows arise from 175 runs, 15 scenario templates, 19 automated systems and 20 human drivers. Bootstrapping runs handles some within-run dependence but not repeated use of the same scenario, system or driver. Flags are concentrated in 120 runs, and the human-versus-AV comparison reuses scenarios across two heterogeneous populations. The central “2.1 times” and “15 of 15 scenarios” statements are shown without uncertainty in Figs. 6a and 6c. Per-endpoint intervals over a frozen threshold battery also do not control family-wise interpretation. This matters because the headline results could be driven by a small subset of scenarios or systems, while overlapping windows make moment counts appear much larger than the independent evidence base. Remedy: use a pre-specified hierarchical model or multiway cluster/bootstrap procedure accounting for scenario and system/driver, report driver-, system- and scenario-level distributions, and perform leave-one-scenario and leave-one-system/driver-out analyses. Give confidence intervals for the flag-rate ratio and paired scenario contrast. Report unique emergency events, not only moments in threshold states, and use simultaneous intervals or a declared multiplicity procedure for the threshold battery.

6. **[Location: Sections 2.1, 4.1–4.4; Fig. 2] — The estimator, reliability gate and reference model are insufficiently specified and stress-tested for a central measurement pipeline.** The PDF does not give the full own-progress and interaction costs, candidate trajectory generator, solver, exact dispersion statistic, full list and timing of situation features, conditional quantile model class, hyperparameters, preprocessing, missing-data handling, or the audit establishing non-anticipation. Referring to code to be released later is not enough for assessing the present claims. The maximum-weight threshold of 0.20, Gaussian scale of 0.1 m, one-second window and seven-point grid could materially determine readability, IPV values and flags. Agreement with an offline estimate generated under the same model is not independent validation. The candidate grid spans only ±3π/8 although Eq. 1 declares a ±π/2 parameter domain, raising possible saturation and boundary effects. In addition, Fig. 2 uses a development split while a confirmatory split is said to remain sealed; the manuscript never reports the confirmatory result. Remedy: make the complete algorithm auditable in the Methods, report the planned confirmatory analysis, and provide sensitivity analyses over grid density/range, likelihood scale, window length, gate threshold, feature sets, map perturbation, tracking noise, frame loss and counterpart-identity switches. Synthetic recovery tests should report error and abstention against known ground truth across the operating envelope.

7. **[Location: Sections 2.3–2.6 and 4.5–4.6; Figs. 4–6] — Appropriate matched baselines are missing.** The global interval demonstrates that conditioning can narrow a reference, but it does not establish that this particular IPV/reference construction is necessary or useful. There is no matched comparison with simple manoeuvre-conditioned quantiles, direct trajectory-likelihood residuals, surprise/expectation monitors, risk-adjusted kinematic rules, or an unconditional/raw-IPV threshold. Without equalizing verdict coverage and abstention, narrower intervals can trivially produce more flags. The consequence association may therefore be available from a simpler observable or may disappear after fair risk matching. For an NMI-level algorithmic contribution, the paper must show what is gained by inverse-planning IPV plus conditional conformal intervals. Remedy: define primary evaluation criteria before comparing methods—calibration, abstention, stability, lead time and incremental consequence association—and evaluate current, tuned baselines under identical splits, inputs and coverage constraints. Include ablations for each gate and reference feature group, not only counterpart IPV and self-history.

8. **[Location: Introduction; Sections 3, 4.4 and 4.6; Algorithm 2] — “Online monitor” is demonstrated only as an offline replay computation and is not yet a runtime-assurance component.** The Methods explicitly state that end-to-end real-time operation on vehicle hardware is not evaluated. No worst-case execution time, deadline, memory demand, stale-input rule, clock alignment, monitor-state semantics or recovery after solver failure is given. The optional persistence signal has no evaluated operating characteristics. The proposed planner actions include a fallback under sustained deviation, yet no arbitration rule ensures that this social signal remains subordinate to a verified collision-safety envelope. “Degraded unverified mode” is especially ambiguous: abstention must not create a control mode that weakens physical-safety enforcement. Remedy: either replace deployment language with an offline-replay proof-of-concept claim, or implement and evaluate a streaming monitor with timing budgets and fault injection. Specify a deterministic state machine for verdicts, abstentions, identity changes and persistence. Any planner interface should be placed inside a runtime-assurance architecture in which an independently justified safety monitor/fallback has priority; define hazards from false positive, false negative, stale and unavailable social signals, and state explicitly that no RSS, reachability, STL or standards-level safety guarantee is supplied.

## 3. Minor issues

1. [Eq. 1 and Fig. 4 legend] Reconcile the declared parameter domain θ ∈ [−π/2, π/2], the seven candidates spanning [−3π/8, 3π/8], and the “admissible range” of 3π/4. Explain how values near the omitted ends are treated.
2. [Eq. 2 and Algorithm 2] Define `q`, `c` and `g` operationally and make abstention reasons mutually exclusive with a stated precedence. The current mapping from `c_i` to `Counterpart-Unreadable` is not self-evident.
3. [Algorithm 2] `signal` is returned even when the optional persistence mapping has not been evaluated or instantiated. It should be omitted or defined as an optional output with explicit state.
4. [Fig. 2a] Define the reliability contrast plotted on the x-axis mathematically, state the control-pair construction, and report the corresponding absolute concentration values. “Case clusters” is too vague.
5. [Fig. 2b] “Readable” and “less readable” require the exact binning rule and uncertainty. The observed greater frame-to-frame movement in the readable group also deserves a sensitivity check for selection on motion magnitude.
6. [Fig. 4] Report the fitted quantile levels, conformal radii and interval-width distributions, not only means. A 95% interval spanning the entire grid suggests an estimator-resolution ceiling that should be discussed.
7. [Fig. 5] Replace “Emergency margins are rarer at every supported threshold” with wording that identifies these as descriptive moment-level associations; an interval excluding zero does not itself make a threshold “supported.”
8. [Fig. 5 and Methods 4.5] State whether threshold events are nested and how repeated samples from one physical event are counted. Give event counts alongside record and moment counts.
9. [Fig. 6] Uncertainty should not be omitted from the two panels supporting the principal human-versus-AV claim. Show intervals and the independent sampling units.
10. [Sections 4.1–4.2] Report the sensor sampling, filtering and time-synchronization assumptions needed to achieve the stated 10 Hz windows, including behaviour under dropped or delayed frames.
11. [Sections 4.3–4.4] Explain how categorical support and k-nearest-neighbour distance interact and whether standardization parameters are frozen from training data only.
12. [Methods and Results] Provide an immutable identifier or dated protocol for each analysis described as “frozen,” and distinguish exploratory, development and confirmatory results consistently.

## 4. Questions to the authors

1. What exact finite-sample or asymptotic coverage statement do the authors claim for accepted moments when calibration scores are dependent within scenes? What is the exchangeable unit?
2. What is the per-run probability of at least one false competitive-deviation flag at the 90% level, and how does it change under each proposed persistence rule?
3. What fractions of all candidate moments in the course-human and automated-system arms fail each gate, and how do the accepted situation distributions differ between the two arms?
4. What are the confidence interval and dependence-aware test for the 9.8% versus 4.7% flag-rate comparison? Does the conclusion survive leave-one-scenario, leave-one-system and leave-one-driver-out analysis?
5. Which exact current and historical variables form `z_t`, at what timestamps are they sampled, and what automated audit establishes that none encodes future passing order or the post-verdict outcome?
6. Does counterpart deceleration or margin contraction begin before the first flag? Please provide aligned pre-trend/event-study plots and analyses with a non-overlapping lag between the estimation and outcome windows.
7. How much incremental information does the flag add beyond current relative kinematics, risk proxies, raw IPV, candidate likelihood, and a global or manoeuvre-conditioned reference at matched judgeability?
8. Why is the confirmatory split mentioned in Fig. 2 still sealed in a completed manuscript? What analyses were committed for it, and when will they be reported?
9. How were the seven-point grid, σ = 0.1 m, maximum-weight threshold 0.20, one-second history and support threshold selected? Were any examined after outcome access?
10. What controlled evidence shows that estimated IPV is monotone in social assertiveness and not merely in trajectory-model mismatch? Are human labels or known-parameter recovery data available?
11. What is the monitor's measured median, tail and worst-case execution time per agent-moment, and what verdict is emitted after a missed deadline, stale map, identity switch or solver failure?
12. In a planner integration, can any social verdict weaken or delay the collision-safety fallback? What formally enforced priority relation prevents this?

## 5. Prioritised revision requests

1. **Validate or narrow the construct.** Add independent IPV/social-meaning validation; otherwise change the title, abstract and claims from social compliance/preference to model-relative conditional human-trajectory atypicality.
2. **Repair the calibration target.** Use scene/interaction-aware conformal calibration and report trace-level false-alarm, persistence and detection-delay operating characteristics, with an exact statement of the guarantee.
3. **Reanalyse the vehicle and human arms at the correct independent units.** Use crossed scenario/system/driver inference, report uncertainty for every headline contrast, multiplicity-aware intervals, and leave-one-group-out robustness.
4. **Establish incremental consequence information.** Add non-overlapping temporal analyses, pre-trends, complete risk adjustment and matched baselines at equal coverage/abstention.
5. **Resolve distribution-shift and selection concerns.** Report gate-by-gate denominators and risk-coverage curves for every domain; either demonstrate target-domain calibration or restrict transfer claims to the tested scenarios.
6. **Make the full estimator and reference model auditable.** Specify features, costs, solver, model class and all hyperparameters; report the sealed confirmatory analysis and robustness to modelling, sensing and support-gate choices.
7. **Substantiate or remove runtime-assurance language.** Measure streaming timing and failure behaviour, define sequential semantics and a safety-prioritized planner interface; otherwise describe the work explicitly as non-anticipatory offline replay.
